# Hypothetical Possibilities
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
A spectator selects a card to represent the "Unknown Future" and it is placed, unseen, into a zippered wallet compartment. A second spectator chooses a card to represent the "Present," signs it, and returns it to the deck. Through a series of time-travel-themed vanishes and reappearances, the signed card repeatedly jumps to the top of the deck despite being placed in the center. The performer then reveals that the first spectator's "Unknown Future" card is an unsigned duplicate of the signed selection — it existed before the signature was on it. The signed card then visually vanishes from the deck (Rub-A-Dub-Dub). A new spectator selects a card via Dribble Force and it is, impossibly, the same card — because "time works in cycles." The signature reappears on this card. Finally, the signed card vanishes from the deck and is discovered zippered inside the wallet — "the Unknown Future always becomes the Present."

## Set-up
- Balducci Wallet (Wonder Wallet / Fred Kaps Wallet style pocket secretary with notepad slide)
- Indelible marker
- A duplicate of the force card (e.g., Two of Hearts, chosen for its large white center)
- Sport jacket required
- Wallet placed in inner left breast pocket along with the marker
- Force card (Two of Hearts) controlled to third from top of the deck
- Duplicate Two of Hearts secretly added to top of deck (positions the Deuces first and fourth)

## Method and Presentation
(*This is an effect dealing with time — past, present, and future*) — false shuffle retaining top four cards. Do a wide face-down ribbon spread: (*To begin, I'm going to ask you to select a card to represent the Unknown Future.*) Spectator touches a card; your right hand prevents him from touching the setup cards on top. Cleanly pull the chosen card out. (*So that it remains unknown, I'm going to place it into the zippered compartment of my wallet.*) Bring out the wallet, unzip, place the chosen card **above the cardboard slide** (not beneath it — the later-loaded card goes on the other side). Zip shut, close wallet, replace it. Before your right hand emerges, **pull up the notepad a few inches so it protrudes above the wallet**. (*We'll get back to it in the future.*)

Square the deck. Begin an Overhand Shuffle, **injog the first card** (which goes on top of the setup), shuffle the balance on top. This positions the injogged card directly above the force card. (*Now I'd like you to choose a card to represent the Present.*) Grasp deck in Biddle Grip, **obtain a break beneath the injogged card**. Execute a **Dribble Force**, dropping cards from about eight inches above the left palm until the spectator says "stop," then release all cards up to the break. (*Look at the card and remember it.*) Extend left hand, he removes the top card. Replace left-hand cards on top of right-hand cards. Table the deck. (*Actually, I'd like you to sign your name on it so that if you look at it at some future date, you'll be reminded of this, what will then be, past event.*) Hand him the marker; he signs across the face. Recap marker, set aside.

Lift deck into dealing position. (*Please place your card on top of the deck.*) As soon as it is placed, **shove over the top two cards slightly, then obtain a left pinky break beneath them as you pull them flush**. Execute a **Double Lift immediately followed by a KM Move**: right hand grasps the double card at center of right long side (thumb above, fingers beneath entering the break), lift about an inch. Left hand turns so deck top faces right. Lower the double until its left long side touches left fingertips. While the lowermost card remains in contact with left fingertips, left hand turns palm down — the lowermost card is pulled, reversed, beneath the deck as the right-hand card is pulled clear. Right hand immediately flips its card (the signed selection) face up. (*At this moment I'd like you to remember this image of your card being on top of the deck.*) Left hand turns palm up while right hand simultaneously lowers the face-up selection onto the deck — timed so the reversed indifferent card beneath is not exposed.

**Flip the double card (face-up selection on top of face-up indifferent card) face down** and immediately deal off the top card. (*We'll get back to it in a moment... after I place it into the middle.*) Insert it into the center. Square freely. (*You know, at this moment, that your card couldn't be on top.*) **Double Turnover** — flip the top two cards face up as one. An indifferent card shows. (*However if we could go back in time... we really can't... but let's just say that we could go back in time to when I asked you to place it on the top...*) Flip the double card face down at that moment. Snap your fingers, flip over the top card to reveal the signed selection: (*...if we could go back to that moment it would then be on top.*)

Thumb off the face-up selection, take it with right hand, turn face down. (*Again, I'll place it into the center...*) Prepare for **Vernon's Depth Illusion**: with left hand alone, raise the inner end of the top card approximately a quarter inch — lift the outer left corner slightly with left thumb, let the break expand inward along the left long side to the thumb base, then press thumb base against the break while relaxing pinky and pressing downward on the outer left corner. The inner end rises evenly while the outer end stays flush. Right hand inserts the face-down selection into the break so it rests flatly on top of the deck. From the front it appears inserted into the center. Square the deck.

(*Obviously, if you turn over the top card, you'll see that it's not on top now...*) Extend hand; spectator turns over the top (indifferent) card. As you extend, **push over the top four cards** with the top card farther over than the others. After he removes and turns over the top card, **left thumb pulls back the three shoved-over cards and obtains a left pinky break beneath them**. Immediately do a **Triple Lift**, gripping the face-up triple at center of its right long side. (*...Nor is it second from the top, third from the top, or even fourth from the top.*) Left thumb pushes off the top card; right hand uses the triple to lever it face up onto the deck, then takes it onto the triple. Repeat with the next card. Left hand takes the spectator's card, places it face up onto the others in right hand ("or even fourth"). Right hand flips the whole mess face down onto the deck, square. (*It however, we could go back in time to when I asked you to place it on top (snap your fingers), it would be on top. Take a look.*) Spectator turns over the top card — signed selection returned.

The face-up selection is on top. Left thumb pushes it and the face-down card beneath it to the right. Right hand (palm down) grasps the face-up selection by center of its right long side, lifts and revolves it face down for a **Top Change**. (*But at this point let us go back in time...*) Left thumb already has the face-down second card right-jogged. Right-hand card lands on top. (*...to just after you picked the card...*) Left thumb holds the top card in place; right hand grips the second card. Left hand extends toward spectator, pointing with third finger, as right hand drops its face-down card on the table in front of him. Left hand tables the deck; right hand picks up the marker. (*...when I handed you the pen.*) Put marker in his hand. (*The reason I handed you the pen was because...*) Pick up the face-down tabled card, hold it between hands, back toward audience. (*...at that point in time, the card didn't have your name on it yet.*) Turn the card around to reveal the unsigned Two of Hearts.

Right hand places the unsigned Deuce face up on the tabled deck. Lift deck into left hand. (*Now, if we could go back to before I handed you the pen, before you even picked the card...*) Thumb off the face-up Deuce, turn it face down, replace on top **jogged to the right about half its width**, held by left thumb. Raise left hand, tilt underside of deck toward audience for one last look at the Deuce. Lower hand. Execute the **Rub-A-Dub-Dub vanish**: left hand descends until the backs of the knuckles and right long side of the jogged card touch the table. Right hand (palm down, fingers together and straight) lowers over the jogged card, allowing its outer right corner to peek between right third and little fingers. Close the window with the pinky; **left thumb pulls the jogged card flush** and that hand immediately moves away turning palm down; right hand flattens onto the table. Rub hand against table, then lift to reveal the vanish. (*...you'll notice that the card wouldn't even be here.*)

Now run through the beginning at double speed. Take lower portion of deck for Overhand Shuffle, **injog the first card**, shuffle balance on top. Biddle Grip, **obtain break beneath injogged card**. Repeat the Dribble Force: (*I'll dribble through the cards, once again say stop wherever you like. Take the card. Look at the card and remember it.*) Extend left hand, thumb off the stopped-at card. Place left-hand cards on top of right-hand cards. Square. Take the unsigned Deuce (spectator has by now turned it face up) with right hand for a **Top Change**. (*You couldn't help but pick the same card since time (Top Change) works in cycles.*) Do the move at "time," left hand gesturing. Right hand drops the face-down card on the table.

(*Of course after you picked the card I handed you the pen.*) Right hand picks up marker, gestures toward spectator, places it between left thumb and first finger held high above the deck. (*The reason I handed you the pen was, of course so that you could sign your name on the card.*) Right hand lifts the face-down card off the table. Left hand makes a signing gesture with the marker over the card. Right hand turns over to reveal the Deuce — the signature is once again on the card. Drop marker, hand card back to spectator. Casually cut the deck to lose the unsigned Deuce. (*Then, after you signed it, I asked you to place it on top...*) Spectator places signed Deuce on top face down. (*...and I placed it into the middle...*) Just mime the action. (*...showed you that it wasn't on top and it wasn't on the bottom.*) Immediately **Double Lift** to display an indifferent card, turn double face down. Lift entire deck with right hand, flash the bottom toward audience.

Turn right hand palm down, replace deck in left-hand dealing position. (*Actually, we could go on like this forever, going forward and back in time, except that it's not possible. However, there is one thing that is possible.*) Execute a **Top Palm**, stealing the signed Deuce into right hand. (*Do you remember, a little while ago, you chose a card to represent the Unknown Future?*) Drop the deck. Left hand pulls left lapel open; right hand enters, **loads the palmed card into the wallet** by pressing it against the cardboard slide and shoving everything downward. In the same motion, grasp the wallet and pull it out. (*I placed it into my wallet.*) Open wallet, gesture toward zipper. Unzip and pull out the face-down card beneath the slide. (*Well, it's true. In fact it's inevitable that the Unknown Future always becomes... the Present.*) Dramatically turn over the card to reveal the signed Two of Hearts.

## Key Notes
- The KM Move: A subtle card reversal where the lowermost card of a double is pulled beneath the deck while the uppermost remains in the right hand. It must be timed so the right-hand card covers the reversed card beneath.
- The Depth Illusion is performed solely with the left hand and requires the card to remain absolutely flat while its inner end is raised. Practice until the card appears genuinely inserted into the center.
- The Rub-A-Dub-Dub vanish relies on precise timing: the left hand pulls the card flush AS the right hand descends, creating the illusion the card is trapped beneath. Timing that seems to "defy printed explanation."
- Throughout, maintain the time-travel conceit — each phase is justified by "going back in time" to a specific moment, motivating each revelation.

## Credit
Inspired by Darwin Ortiz's "The Dream Card" (Darwin Ortiz at the Card Table, 1988), which is in turn a disguised version of Alex Elmsley's "Between Your Palms." The Rub-A-Dub-Dub vanish is from Hugard and Braue's Expert Card Technique (1940).

# Inside/Outside
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
Two blank pieces of cardstock are shown. The performer writes "INSIDE" on the upper surface of each, then initials one while the spectator initials the other. The two cards are stapled together with the words "INSIDE" on the outside. Instantly, through a flourish, the words vanish and both sides of the stapled unit appear completely blank. When the staples are pulled open and the cards peeled apart, the words "INSIDE" are discovered on the inside surfaces — exactly where their meaning dictates they should be. The cards are turned so the words are once again on the outside and a flame is waved beneath them. Upon examination, the cards have fused together into a single card bearing "INSIDE" and both sets of initials on both sides.

## Set-up
- Two double blank cards (preferred over regular playing cards — laymen do not assume they are blank playing cards)
- Stapler
- Indelible marker
- Cigarette lighter (in right-side jacket or trousers pocket)
- In advance: write "INSIDE" in bold capital letters on one side of one card, with your initials beneath. Place this card, written side downward, beneath the other blank card.
- Gary carries a stack of prepared pairs in a left-side pocket so the trick can be repeated.

## Method and Presentation
Bring out the pair, holding them in left-hand dealing position with the written side of the lower card facing your palm. Left thumb spreads off the upper card; right hand turns palm down to take it at center of its right long side (thumb beneath, fingers above). Right hand takes the upper card and turns palm up, revolving the card end over end — it lands back on top of the card in your left hand. Left thumb immediately descends to hold the top card. **Right fingertips nip the right long side of the lower card and draw it to the right.** When almost completely exposed, **use it to flip over the card remaining in the left hand.** Done smoothly with no pauses, this shows both sides of both cards as blank (Larry Jennings sleight). Drop right-hand card on top and square.

(*Two blank pieces of cardboard, upon which I will write a meaningful word in the English language.*) Bring out the marker; write "INSIDE" on the upper surface of the top card in large letters. (*The word 'INSIDE'...*) Display, then thumb off the top card onto the table. Write "INSIDE" on the second card: (*...about as meaningful to most of you as 'OUTSIDE,' 'AROUND,' or 'BETWEEN,' I realize, but you'll soon see why I'm using this word and not another.*) Begin to recap, but interrupt: (*Let's give this a personal touch. I'll initial this one...*) Initial the card in hand — it now looks identical to the underside of the tabled card. Flash the blank back. Give the marker to the spectator; at the same time, left hand uses its card (written side upward) to **scoop up the tabled card**. Square; spectator initials the uppermost card directly beneath "INSIDE."

(*At this point, the logical thing to do would be to put the 'INSIDES' on the inside so the words occupy the positions dictated by their meaning.*) Right hand grasps the packet by center of right long side (thumb above, fingers beneath); left thumb **draws the card with the spectator's initials onto the left palm**. Right hand very slowly turns its card over so the written surfaces are face to face, and lowers it onto the left-hand card.

(*But, we're going to defy the laws of nature and epistemology and put the 'INSIDES' on the outside.*) Right hand regrips the packet at center of right long side and lifts it out of left hand. **Left thumb draws off the top card** onto left palm. Place right-hand card squarely on top. Lift in Biddle Grip and flash both sides — your initials seen on the underside, spectator's initials on the upper side. Replace into left hand but do not let go. Immediately **Side Steal the lower card into right hand and load it on top**: move right hand completely over the packet, plant the stolen card in place as left hand tilts slightly toward you. Right hand grasps the outer end (thumb above, fingertips wrapped around). **Lift the packet and revolve it face down** back into left-hand dealing position — the end-over-end turn shades the blank side of the stolen card.

At this point the card order is: upper card top surface has your initials and bottom surface has spectator's initials; lower card top surface has your initials and bottom surface is blank.

Bring out the stapler. (*And to keep them on the outside, I'll staple them together.*) Staple the inner end, then reach around to staple the outer end — during this, **turn right hand palm down (stapler upside down) so the second staple is attached prongs upward**. This disguises the later turnover.

Hold left hand palm up, packet resting on outstretched fingers. Execute **Vernon's Through the Fist Flourish**: close fingers and simultaneously turn left hand palm down — the packet remains same side up (your initials visible). **Push it out the far side of the fist with left thumb**, taking it with right hand. Immediately turn left hand palm up, open it. Lower packet onto left palm. Again close fingers and turn hand over — **this time the packet is turned over inside the hand**, so when pushed out the far side, the blank side is upward. (*The attraction to the inside is so great, that the outside becomes the inside.*) The visible vanish of both signatures is startling.

Right hand takes the packet as it emerges. Left hand turns palm up. Return packet to dealing position, pause. Execute **The Unidirectional Turnover**: right hand descends as if to take Biddle Grip but instead **the outer end is nipped between right second and third fingers** (first and second fingertips below, third and little fingertips above; thumb hovers behind). First and second fingers press downward, third and little fingers press upward — **this instantaneously flips the packet over sideways** while the right hand simultaneously turns the packet end over end. Both sides appear blank.

Starting at the outer left corner, **pull the cards apart**, ripping the staples open when you reach them, to reveal "INSIDE" and the initials on the inner surfaces of both cards. Pause. Pull cards completely apart and pick out both staples: (*Obviously staples are no hindrance to the power of the word.*) Keep the lower card flatly on left palm so its underside (duplicate writing) is not exposed.

(*But there is a more effective way of keeping the inside on the outside — it's called fusion.*) Take the upper card, turn it over so "INSIDE" and your initials are on the underside, place it beneath the card in left hand. Grasp both in right-hand Biddle Grip, lift out, flash the underside, revolve palm down. Left hand pats pocket as if looking for the lighter. As you apparently replace the packet, do a **Side Steal, palming the lower card in right hand**. Left hand, holding only one card, immediately turns palm down to flash writing on the underside — audience believes they see two cards. Left hand turns palm up. Right hand goes to pocket, drops the palmed card, and brings out the lighter. Hand lighter to spectator, ask him to click it on.

Right hand descends over the single card in left hand in Biddle Grip. Execute a variation of **Paul Harris' Snap Count**: right thumb lifts the inner end of the card slightly; left pinky snaps off the inner right corner. Immediately, right thumb lets the inner end snap off it. This creates two clicks and looks/sounds like riffling two cards. Repeat a second time.

Lift the card with right hand, wave it over the flame, replace in left hand. Squash your hands flatly together, trapping the card between. Slowly separate hands, lift the card, and display that the cards have fused into a single card with printing on both sides. Give it to the spectator as a souvenir.

## Key Notes
- The Jennings turnover at the beginning must be executed smoothly with no pauses whatsoever to convincingly show both sides of both cards blank.
- The Side Steal's end-over-end cover turn is critical — it shades the blank surface of the stolen card during the critical load.
- Stapling the outer end prongs-upward is a small but vital detail: after the secret turnover, the staples will still appear one-up and one-down rather than both flipped.
- The Through the Fist Flourish must be practiced until the packet turnover is invisible. The first pass (no turnover) establishes the normal action; the second pass (with turnover) creates the vanish.
- The Unidirectional Turnover's mechanics require the packet to be nipped precisely between the specified fingers for an instantaneous snap-flip.
- The Snap Count variation avoids the need for a second card; the left pinky snap followed by the thumb release convincingly simulates two cards clicking.

## Credit
Inspired by Jay Sankey's "Inside Out" from Up For Adoption (1987 lecture notes). The Unidirectional Turnover was created independently by Gary, though its initial action is identical to a small packet reverse of Dai Vernon's (Larry Jennings' The Cardwright, Maxwell, 1988). Paul Harris' Snap Count variation used at the climax.

# Constant Return
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
A borrowed quarter and the performer's quarter (marked "MINE") are used. Despite the performer openly exchanging his "MINE" quarter for the spectator's unmarked quarter, "MINE" always returns to the performer's hand. In Part Two, the performer drops the MINE quarter onto the spectator's hand — the instant it touches, the word "MINE" visibly vanishes from it and the spectator now holds an unmarked quarter while "MINE" is back in the performer's hand. In Part Three, a final exchange proves equally impossible as the coins continue to switch places.

## Set-up
- Two quarters
- Indelible marker
- Write "MINE" on both sides of one quarter; place this into the left front trousers pocket
- Write "MINE" on one side of the other quarter; **classic palm this in the right hand, written side away from the palm**

## Method and Presentation

**Part One:** (*I'd like to show you an amazing thing with something of mine and something of yours. Do you have a quarter?*) Borrow the quarter with left hand. (*This is what we'll use of yours.*) Transfer the borrowed quarter to right hand, taking it onto the second and third fingers near the tips. As left hand goes to the trousers pocket and brings out the double-sided MINE quarter, **right hand turns palm down, fingers naturally curling. The borrowed quarter is Back Clipped between right second and third fingers** (pushed between them by the thumb, about half an inch from fingertips). Once the borrowed quarter is behind the fingers, relax palm and **let the one-sided MINE quarter drop from classic palm onto the second and third fingers, over the Back-Clipped coin**.

Left hand brings out the double-sided MINE quarter and shows both sides: (*This is what we'll use of mine.*) Right hand turns palm up, displaying the unmarked side of a quarter on the fingers. (*Now you might think that the reason I wrote 'MINE' on mine was because I was afraid of mixing our quarters up, quite the contrary. It's to show you that we could never mix our quarters up. I'll keep your quarter. You can take mine, please hold out your hand, palm up.*) **Openly exchange**: slide the MINE quarter beneath the unmarked quarter on right fingertips, then pull away the unmarked quarter with left hand. Turn right hand palm down, curling fingers inward, **pressing the MINE quarter into classic palm**. Right thumb reaches behind the fingers and **grips the Back-Clipped borrowed quarter**, straighten fingers (thumb drags the quarter inside), extend right hand and press the coin onto spectator's fingers, closing them into a fist. Turn his hand palm down.

Maneuver the apparently unmarked quarter onto left palm. (*But this condition can never last long because mine always comes back to me.*) Close left hand into a fist and turn it over, **secretly flipping over the coin** (using the technique from "Body Heat"). Immediately turn left fist palm up, open to reveal the MINE side upward. Right hand settles into a loose palm-down fist; allow the classic-palmed double-sided MINE quarter to drop to fingertip rest. A moment after left hand opens, do a **Roth Shuttle Pass**, apparently dumping the left-hand quarter into the right hand. Left hand retains the coin in finger palm (turning palm down); right hand turns palm up, its fingers carrying the double MINE coin beneath. Bring right-hand coin into view, flash both sides. (*And if you open your hand you'll see that yours always ends up back with you.*) Spectator finds an unmarked quarter.

**Part Two:** This sequence is based on Geoffrey Latta's "Copper/Silver No.4." Take the spectator's quarter with right hand. At this point: quarter with "MINE" on one side is in left-hand finger palm (writing away from hand); double MINE quarter between left thumb and fingertips; borrowed quarter between right thumb and fingertips. Both hands palm up. Openly place the unmarked quarter into left hand (fingers open and close slightly to accept it — do not flash the finger-palmed coin).

(*Once again, I'll keep your coin, and you'll get my coin. Please hold out your hand.*) Right hand takes the double MINE coin from between left thumb and first finger, maneuvering it onto the first and second fingers of the palm-up right hand, held just to the right of the spectator's palm. Left fist is held just to the left, almost directly over the spectator's hand, in position for **Latta's handling of Han Ping Chien into a spectator's hand**. (*Watch as I drop mine onto your hand, because the instant it touches, it becomes yours.*) **Raise right third finger, lower it onto the overlapping portion of the MINE quarter, press downward so the coin flops over and is clipped between second and third fingers.** Simultaneously: right hand turns palm down directly over spectator's hand; left fist allows the unmarked quarter to drop out the pinky side as it glides to the left. The word MINE appears to visibly vanish the moment it touches the spectator's hand.

Quickly extend right thumb and first finger, turn over the unmarked quarter on spectator's hand to display both sides. Slowly open left hand to reveal the one-sided MINE quarter: (*That leaves mine in my hand.*)

**Part Three:** Turn left hand palm down, curling fingers so the one-sided MINE quarter drops onto them. Move thumb onto the uppermost (unmarked) side; straighten thumb and fingers — this keeps the MINE side upward. Place it onto and overlapping the quarter in the spectator's hand.

Pause. Lower palm-down left hand over the coins on spectator's hand and **lift both, keeping them concealed under the fingers**. Raise left hand, turn palm toward you. Right hand comes over and **pulls out the innermost coin** — this is the one-sided MINE quarter, now unmarked side toward audience. (*One last time. I'll keep yours, and give you mine.*) Left hand moves forward, **presses its coin behind the fingers flatly onto the spectator's hand**. Left hand crosses over in front of you (partially shielding right hand for just a moment). In that instant, **right thumb and first finger spin the quarter around so the MINE side is toward the audience**. (*As soon as I let go of mine, it becomes yours.*) Lift left hand to reveal the unmarked quarter on spectator's hand. Audience looks at right hand — "MINE" is written on it.

**Cleanup:** Right thumb pulls the one-sided MINE quarter inward until its lower edge is clipped between second and third fingers. Left hand turns palm up; right hand turns palm down, releasing the double-sided MINE coin from right-hand finger palm onto left hand. The one-sided MINE coin flops over and is retained clipped. Cleanly display the MINE quarter on both sides: (*I'll just have to keep mine, and you'll just have to keep yours.*)

## Key Notes
- The Back Clip is not a Deep Back Clip — the coin is about half an inch from the fingertips, making it secure yet easily accessible.
- The open exchange sequence at the end of Part One is crucial: the audience sees you slide the coins apart in what appears to be a perfectly fair exchange.
- The Latta/Han Ping Chien sequence in Part Two must be precisely timed: the right hand turns palm down, the left hand glides left releasing the coin, and the right third finger clips the MINE quarter — all simultaneously.
- The coin spin at the end of Part Three is a vital misdirection moment, concealed by the natural crossing motion of the left hand.
- The entire routine is designed to be performed while standing (Back Clipping is involved), making it ideal for cocktail parties and walkaround situations.

## Credit
Part Two is based on Geoffrey Latta's "Copper/Silver No.4" from CoinMagic (Kaufman, 1981). The Han Ping Chien into spectator's hand handling is from CoinMagic (1981). The Roth Shuttle Pass is from David Roth's Expert Coin Magic. The coin flip technique in Part One references "Body Heat" (elsewhere in Unexplainable Acts).

# Attraction
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
The performer borrows the finger rings of a couple and threads them onto a pencil, which the couple then holds horizontally between them. The performer backs several feet away and, with no apparent physical connection to the rings or the pencil, the gentleman's ring begins to slowly creep across the pencil toward the woman's ring until the two touch. The performer dramatically passes his hands over the pencil during the movement. The rings are then returned to the couple.

## Set-up
- A pencil
- Pink putty
- A foot-and-a-half length of invisible thread
- Remove the eraser from the pencil and replace it with the pink putty, into which one end of the invisible thread has been rolled. Carefully mold the putty to look exactly like a worn-down eraser.
- Tie the other end of the invisible thread to the buckle on your belt.
- Place the pencil, point side downward, into the left breast pocket of your shirt.

## Method and Presentation
(*Being the man that I am... a helpless romantic... I'd like to demonstrate for you the power of love.*) Single out a gentleman: (*You heard me right...*) Shift gaze to a woman: (*...the power of love. Do we have any couples in the audience tonight? A show of hands from the happily attached — great.*) Choose a couple both wearing rings, ask their names, and invite them up. (*With your permission, I'd like to use your finger rings, and there's no need to worry because I practically won't touch them.*) Take the pencil from your pocket with right hand, turn it horizontal (eraser end to the right). Take it by the point with left hand.

Take the gentleman's ring with right hand, **slip it onto the right (eraser) end of the pencil**, letting it slide down until it touches left fingertips. Take the woman's ring and slide it onto the pencil the same way. **The rings encircle both the pencil and the invisible thread.**

Ask the gentleman to lightly grasp the pencil by its point, and the woman to grasp the other end (guiding her fingers to hold the metal portion of the eraser end). Let go completely. **Gently slide the woman's ring all the way to the right until it is beside her fingertips.** Instruct them to hold the pencil perfectly level, simultaneously taking their hands to prevent movement. (*In every relationship there is a dominant partner. Which of you is the dominant person?*) Allow for amusing banter. (*Well, this is the test. Please hold the pencil perfectly still.*)

Direct attention to the rings and **begin slowly backing away** — the gentleman's ring will creep across the pencil to join the woman's ring. During the movement, **dramatically pass your hands over the pencil** — this shades the invisible thread, preventing any light from reflecting off it. Stop as soon as the rings touch and take hold of their hands. (*Truly the power of love and attraction.*)

With left hand, take the left end of the pencil from the man. Ask the woman to also let go. Hold your palm-up right hand beneath the right end of the pencil and **dump both rings onto it**. Hand them back to the spectators and thank them. Take the pencil with right hand, replace it in the left breast pocket of your shirt.

## Key Notes
- Scouting the audience during earlier tricks is advisable to confirm that there is at least one couple wearing rings.
- The positioning of the woman's ring at the far right end before backing away ensures maximum travel distance for the gentleman's ring, enhancing the visual drama.
- Passing hands over the pencil during the ring's movement is not merely dramatic — it is mechanically essential to prevent ambient light from catching the invisible thread.
- The effect is endlessly repeatable due to the simplicity of the hookup — perfect for restaurant or walkaround work.
- For close-up or walkaround situations where the audience is sitting, go to the couple and, if possible, sit between them rather than bringing them forward.

# Wishful Thinking
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
A spectator selects a card, signs it, and loses it in the deck. The performer asks the spectator to choose where the card should appear: top, middle, or bottom. The spectator names a position, and the card is found there. This is repeated twice more so the card appears in all three positions at the spectator's choosing. Finally, the performer states his own wish -- that the card should be in his pocket -- and removes the signed selection from that location. An optional extra climax has the signature vanish from the card.

## Set-up
- An ordinary deck and an indelible marker
- Optional: a duplicate of the force card (signed by spectator) with letraline applied per David Williamson's "peeling off the signature" technique, and an unsigned duplicate for a Top Change
- A Balducci/Kaps Wallet or Le Paul/Jennings Wallet if producing the card from a wallet for the final revelation
- If using the wallet climax: the wallet in inner left breast pocket; if using pocket production: trousers loose enough for Gordon Bruce's thumb-and-first-finger technique

## Method and Presentation
(*We all, from time to time, practice wishful thinking. Sometimes it's fruitful, and other times it just leads to more wishful thinking. I'm going to create a situation in which your wishes just might be granted.*) Spread the face-down deck for a selection. Have the card signed and returned, controlling it to the top via Le Paul's Spread Pass or any preferred method.

(*Tell me where in the deck you want your card to appear -- top, middle, or bottom. You name the spot and that's where it will be. Your wish is my command.*)

**On the Top:** (*It's not on top now, is it?*) **Do a Double Lift** to show an indifferent card. Turn the double face down and place the deck on the spectator's outstretched palm. (*I snap my fingers and your wish is granted. Take a look.*)

**In the Middle:** (*In the middle?*) **Do a Double Lift** to show it is not on top, turning the double face down. (*Well, it's not on top.*) Lift the deck in Biddle Grip, turn palm up to flash the face card. (*And it's not on the bottom.*) Turn palm down, replace deck in left hand. (*It must be in the middle!*) Execute Gary's **Slip Force**: riffle down the outer left corner with the left thumb until the spectator says stop. Raise all cards above that point bookwise to a vertical position, the right long sides of both halves still touching. Hold the upper half upright with the left thumb. The left fingertips press against the top card of the upper half. The right hand reaches around as if to grasp the outer end of the upper half, but does not yet grip it. Squeeze the left fingertips against the back of the top card, angling the face of the half deck slightly toward the audience -- the top card is held in place by the left fingertips as the cards beneath it move forward. The right hand firmly grasps the outer end of the upper half, swinging it forward until the top card snaps flatly onto the lower half. Immediately turn the right hand palm down and use its cards (now face up) to tap the top of the left-hand cards. The spectator turns over the card at that point to reveal the selection.

**On the Bottom:** (*On the bottom?*) Execute Gary's **Top to Bottom Pivot Palm** (variation of Cliff Green's Imperceptible Top to Bottom Palm). Shift the deck forward in the left hand so the inner left corner will be the pivot point. The left thumb lies along the left side. The right hand gestures, then returns -- the right thumb lands about a half-inch to the right of the inner left corner of the top card, first finger curled lightly on top, other fingers wrapped around the outer end. The right thumb pushes diagonally inward and to the left, causing the top card to pivot into palm position. Without pausing, the right first and second fingers clip the outer end of the deck and pull it forward. The right thumb moves to the inner end. Lift the deck in Biddle Grip, turn palm up to show the bottom. (*It's not on the bottom now, is it?*) Reverse the actions, turning the right hand palm down and placing the deck back into the left hand. As the deck lands, do the **Bottom Palm Replacement**: the left pinky tip scoots around to the inner side of the palmed card, other fingers relax, allowing the curled card to spring out flat while rotating so the end that was toward the right turns outward. The left first finger pushes the turning card up against the bottom of the deck. (*Please hold out your hand.*) Place the deck on the spectator's palm. (*Take a look.*) He lifts the deck and turns it over to reveal the signed card on the bottom.

Between each of the three wishes, the selection must apparently be lost in the deck. If the card was on the bottom: take the deck back, turn it face down, and Double Cut the card from bottom to top. If in the middle: have the card replaced in the center, toss the upper half onto it maintaining a left pinky break above, and Double Cut to the top. If on top: replace the selection face down on top, cut holding a break above it, and Double Cut back to the top.

(*That was making it appear wherever you wish -- but sometimes it's nice to make it appear wherever I wish, and right now I wish your card was in my pocket.*) Execute Gary's **Fingertip Longitudinal Steal**: insert the card into the center of the deck, secretly injogging it. The right hand grasps the inner end of the deck, thumb above, first and second fingers beneath, third finger covering the injogged portion. Lift the deck and rotate it so the face is toward the audience, at the same time turning the left hand to an identical position -- a stunning display because nothing appears to be under control. Turn both hands palm up and bring them together. The right third finger, maintaining light upward pressure, curls inward, swinging the injogged card out of the deck and into Longitudinal Palm (the card rotates until at right angles to the deck, left end held by right thumb, right end against the heel of the right hand). Produce the card from a pocket, wallet, or sealed envelope.

(*Optional climax:*) (*I wish that your signature was not on my card -- it ruins the deck.*) With a duplicate card setup (signed selection on top, unsigned duplicate third from top), after the card has appeared in all three positions, leave the spectator holding the signed selection. Double Cut the top card to the bottom, bringing the unsigned duplicate to the top. Take the selection and do a **Top Change**. Rub the face of the card on the table or trousers, then turn it over to reveal the signature has been wiped off. For the strongest finish, prepare the duplicate with letraline so the signature can be "peeled off" (per David Williamson's "51 Cards to Pocket").

## Key Notes
- The three contingency handlings must be executed without hesitation since the spectator's choice is unknown in advance
- The Fingertip Longitudinal Steal is the standout technical moment: the injogged card is held by the right third finger during the open display, then curled inward to swing the card into Longitudinal Palm in a flash
- The Slip Force shares conceptual DNA with the handling in "Hypothetical Possibilities" earlier in the book, but here Gary's version is taught in full detail
- When producing the palmed card from a trousers pocket, Gary recommends Gordon Bruce's technique of inserting only the thumb and first finger (carrying the concealed card) while the other fingers remain outside the pocket
- If the spectator does not request all three positions in typical sequence, simply name the remaining position and ask them to request it ("You haven't asked me to make it appear in the middle yet, so ask me now")

## Credit
- Le Paul's Spread Pass (control)
- Cliff Green's Imperceptible Top to Bottom Palm (*Professional Card Magic*, 1961) -- basis for Gary's variation
- David Williamson's "peeling off the signature" climax (*Williamson's Wonders*, 1989)
- Gordon Bruce's pocket production technique

# Split Disclosure
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
A spectator is handed a sealed coin envelope and told to place it in his pocket. A deck surrounded by a rubber band is held face up by the performer, who riffles up the outer end until the spectator says stop. All cards above the chosen one are pulled back slightly, enabling the spectator to sign the outer end of his chosen card. The deck is squared and dropped onto the table with obviously empty hands. The performer brings out a wallet; inside its zippered compartment is a sealed envelope. The spectator tears open his envelope to discover half of his chosen card. The performer tears open his envelope, which contains the other, signed, half. The jagged tears match perfectly.

## Set-up
- A Le Paul/Jennings Card in Envelope Wallet (loaded with a sealed envelope), placed in inner left breast pocket
- A deck of cards with a wide rubber band around it
- A small coin envelope, sealed
- An indelible marker
- Preparation: remove one spot card from the deck. Tear it jaggedly in half. Place one half inside the coin envelope and seal it. Place the other half in the deck near the outer end, then place the rubber band around the deck (slightly closer to the outer end to hold the half card more securely). The half card in the deck must be injogged a tiny bit.

## Method and Presentation
Hold the pre-banded deck face up in left-hand dealing position. Bring out the sealed coin envelope and hand it to the spectator. (*This contains a prediction.*) Execute a **Riffle Force** by riffling up the outer end with the right fingers. The injogged half card causes you to stop automatically. Time the riffle with the spectator's verbal command so he says "stop" when you reach the half card. Injog all the cards above the half card about one inch and hand the marker to the spectator. (*Please initial your card.*)

After he has finished, recap the marker and place it aside. Rotate the deck so it remains face up and the end with the half card is toward you. The right hand descends over the deck in Biddle Grip, the right thumbtip landing on the signed half card. Slide the upper half of the deck inward until flush with the lower half, at the same time **injogging the half card with the right thumb**.

Take the deck with the right hand in position for Gary's **Fingertip Longitudinal Steal** (from "Wishful Thinking"), the right third finger lying across the injogged half card during the open display. Bring the hands together, executing the steal and palming the half card in the right hand. Once the deck has been placed into the left hand, turn it palm down and drop the cards onto the table.

The right hand enters the jacket, loads the half card into the wallet, and brings the wallet into view. Open the wallet, unzip the side, and reach inside, pulling out the loaded envelope. Place the wallet back in the pocket. (*Please tear open your envelope and look at the prediction.*) Once it has registered that he has half of his chosen card, tear open your envelope and reveal the other, signed, half. Fit the halves together, showing that the jagged tears match perfectly.

## Key Notes
- The injogged half card serves double duty: it acts as an automatic stop for the Riffle Force and provides a grip point for the Fingertip Longitudinal Steal
- The rubber band being closer to the outer end both secures the half card and serves as a tactile reminder of which end contains the gimmick
- The open display before the steal -- with the third finger hiding the injogged portion -- looks impossibly clean because the deck appears freely held

## Credit
- Paul Harris, "Guts" (*Close-Up Fantasies Finale*, 1981) -- the concept of a card torn in half to be in two places at once
- Gary Kurtz's Fingertip Longitudinal Steal (described in "Wishful Thinking")

# Body Heat
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
The performer brings out a change purse and removes three different-sized copper coins from it: a jumbo penny, an English Penny, and a Mexican 20 Centavo. Two spectators are each given a coin to hold, while the performer holds the third. Through the power of "body heat," all three copper coins change into silver -- the jumbo penny becomes a silver dollar, the English Penny becomes a half dollar, and the Centavo becomes a quarter. The changes occur without any visible false moves.

## Set-up
- A silver dollar, a large (jumbo) penny (also silver dollar size), a quarter, a small change purse large enough to conceal the silver dollar beneath it
- A Connie Hayden "Two Copper One Silver" gimmick: an English Penny hollowed out so a Mexican 20 Centavo fits inside; when the Centavo is pressed inside the back of the Penny, you have a copper/silver coin showing the Penny on one side and a half dollar on the other
- Place the quarter inside the English Penny shell. Place that unit on top of the jumbo penny. Place the Centavo on top. All three coins go inside the purse, snapped shut. Place the silver dollar on top of the purse and slip everything into the left front trousers pocket. Orient so the jumbo penny is farthest from you, the silver dollar on the outward side of the purse.

## Method and Presentation
(*Have you ever heard of alchemy? It is a combination of science and magic practiced by people who are obsessed with changing base metal into gold. Well, I'm a bit of an alchemist myself. What I'm going to show you is the effect of heat on metal.*) Insert the left hand into the trousers pocket, cupping the purse inside curled fingers with the silver dollar in finger palm. Bring out the purse. (*When I say heat you might think of the hot Arizona sun at noon, or the flame of a candle, or a burning fireplace on a cold winter night. But I'm talking about body heat: ninety-eight point six degrees Fahrenheit.*) The left hand turns palm up, the purse lying innocently on the open hand -- the silver dollar concealed beneath.

Tilt the left palm toward you and snap open the purse with the right hand. Reach inside and pull out the jumbo penny, dropping it onto a spectator's hand. (*The coins we're going to use are copper. The first one is a rather large penny, worth absolutely nothing, despite its size.*) The left thumb snaps the purse shut. The right hand returns to the purse, lifts it off the left hand, and takes it away -- the silver dollar remains behind in left-hand finger palm. The left hand rotates palm toward you, fingers naturally curling, as the purse is removed.

Take the jumbo penny back from the spectator with the right hand and transfer it to the left. (*I'd like you to hold out your right hand, palm down.*) Demonstrate with the right hand. Once the spectator follows, the right hand takes the jumbo penny from the left, and both hands turn palm down. Execute a **Fingertip Retention Vanish** (per Roth's *Expert Coin Magic*, page 9) -- the left hand turns palm up and closes into a fist after the fake placement. (*Squeeze the coin tightly.*) The right thumb and first finger tug the left sleeve up as misdirection while the right second finger pushes the jumbo penny into classic palm. Turn the left hand palm down, letting the silver dollar drop to fingertip rest. Move the left hand beneath the spectator's and straighten the fingers, placing the dollar under her hand and pressing it against her palm. Press down on her fingers to cue her to close them into a fist.

The right hand lifts the purse. Let the jumbo penny fall from classic palm to fingertip rest, then straighten the fingers, carrying the jumbo penny beneath the purse. Place the purse onto the palm-up left hand, penny concealed beneath. (*I also have two other coins, also copper, and also worth next to nothing.*) Tilt the left hand toward you and snap open the purse with the right hand. Withdraw the remaining contents: the English Penny and Centavo in front of it -- they emerge copper sides toward the audience. The left thumb presses the purse shut and returns it (with the concealed jumbo penny) to the left front trousers pocket. The right thumb holds the quarter firmly inside the English Penny shell.

The now-empty left hand returns to the right hand (both palm up) and takes the Centavo. (*An English Penny, worth nothing since it's no longer in circulation, and a Mexican 20 Centavo -- worthless for obvious reasons.*) The right third finger moves beneath the lower edge of the English Penny and the thumb relaxes slightly, allowing the quarter to tilt inward. The left hand slips the Centavo partially in front of the quarter -- the copper sides of the coins remain toward the audience throughout. Press firmly with the right thumb. Move the left hand away and gesture palm down as you ask a different spectator to hold out her hand palm down. After the gesture, the left hand returns. The left thumb pushes the Centavo completely behind the English Penny, and once the coins line up, the Centavo slips inside the English Penny. The left hand takes the English Penny/Centavo combination, allowing it to remain in view beyond the fingertips. That leaves the quarter hidden behind the right fingertips. Press the quarter up beneath the second spectator's outstretched palm-down hand, on the finger base (this prevents her from detecting the size difference). (*I'd like you to hold the twenty Mexican centavos, and I'll keep the penny.*)

The right hand takes the English Penny and lays it on the center of the left palm in display position. Close the left fingers into a fist, contracting the palm and pushing against the coin with the thumb base -- this causes the coin to rise to a vertical position. The left fingers then pull it over, silver side upward, as they close and the left fist turns over. Wriggle the left fingers, maneuvering the coin back to the same spot on the palm. (*We'll allow body heat to work on those coins, like time working on an investment in a bank, increasing their value, changing their substance from copper to silver...from copper to silver. Would you open your hand?*) The woman holding the quarter opens her hand to reveal silver. Open the left hand revealing the half dollar, then ask the other spectator to open her hand to reveal the silver dollar. Gather the three coins, bring out the purse, and put them back inside to end.

## Key Notes
- The "Two Copper One Silver" gimmick is the core apparatus; the nested Centavo-inside-Penny with half-dollar back is what enables the seemingly impossible transformation
- The coin-flip in the fist (contracting the palm and using the thumb base to lever the coin vertical) is Gary's method for cleanly turning the English Penny over so the half-dollar side shows. Practice this single move extensively
- The Fingertip Retention Vanish for the jumbo penny (page 9 of Roth's *Expert Coin Magic*) must be smooth -- the tugging of the sleeve provides the misdirection for the palm
- The rhythm of the routine builds suspense through the two spectators each holding an unrevealed coin while the performer's coin changes first
- This is designed for standing performance with a small group (two to several people)

## Credit
- Connie Hayden, "Two Copper One Silver" gimmick
- David Roth's Fingertip Retention Vanish (*Expert Coin Magic*)
- Dai Vernon's Copper/Silver handling (inspirational basis)

# Four-Fisted
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
The performer removes a small purse from his pocket and pulls out two English Pennies, noting they have different dates. The pennies are mixed and one is placed into each of the spectator's hands -- the premise being to guess which penny is in which hand. The performer states this would not be a very impressive feat. He pulls two more pennies from the purse, remarking they have no real value. It would be a better trick, he continues, if he could change them into coins of greater value -- and the two pennies instantly change into half dollars. The performer asks the spectator to open his hands to see if his coins have also changed, but the spectator finds a gold coin in each hand. Looking back at the magician, he sees that he is also holding two gold coins.

## Set-up
- Four gold-colored coins, two English Pennies with different dates, two copper/silver coins (English Penny on one side, half dollar on the other), and a small purse
- All eight coins must be approximately the same diameter
- Place the four gold coins into the front left pocket of the trousers
- Into the purse: place the copper/silver coins together (both copper sides facing outward), and the genuine pennies in front of those. Snap the purse shut and place it into the same pocket as the gold coins.

## Method and Presentation
(*I'm going to show you the difference between a good trick and a bad trick. Well, for you, between good, better, and best.*) The left hand reaches into the pocket, finger palms two of the four gold coins, then grasps the purse and comes out. Display the purse on the hand, covering the palmed coins. Lower the thumb onto the purse and tilt the left hand toward you. Snap open the purse with the right hand. Look inside and bring out the two genuine pennies, dropping them onto the spectator's hand. Snap the purse shut and place it back into the left front trousers pocket. (The left hand now has two gold coins finger palmed.)

Pick up one penny and read its date aloud. Ask the spectator to name the date on the penny he is still holding. Take the penny from him, so you hold both, and begin mixing them at the fingertips. Turn your head away while doing this, peeking every so often for comic effect. (*I'm going to mix them up so I don't know which is which. Trust me?*) Take both pennies between the left thumb and fingers so the right hand can gesture palm up. (*Hold out both of your hands...*) As soon as the spectator puts out his hands (palm up, because that is how your right hand is held), say, (*Oh, sorry, I mean palm down.*) Immediately turn the right hand palm down. The spectator, confused and faintly embarrassed, turns his hands over. During this misdirective moment, execute Gary's **Take Two Switch**: the left hand ascends and presses the pennies into classic palm in the right hand. The right thumb then moves onto the innermost gold coin in left-hand finger palm and begins pulling it to the right -- that coin is dragged away inside the right fingers while the other gold coin remains in the left hand, the left thumb pushing it to just inside the fingertips. Both hands now hold gold coins in identical positions, hidden behind the fingers.

Move the right hand beneath the spectator's left hand, and the left hand beneath his right. Each hand pushes a gold coin against his fingers, and your thumbs curl his fingers around it into a fist. He now believes he holds copper pennies.

(*Would you be impressed if I could tell which hand holds the 1962 penny?*) Innocently clasp your hands together, right (palm down) over left (palm up), and allow one of the pennies to drop out of right-hand classic palm onto the left fingers. (*Not really? It's not a very good trick. It's more of a guessing game, a fifty-fifty chance.*) Separate the hands, the left hand turning palm down with the penny in finger palm. Insert the left hand into the front trousers pocket. Inside the pocket, grasp the two gold coins between the left thumb and first finger, allow the finger-palmed penny to drop, transfer the two gold coins to finger palm, then grasp the purse, pulling it over the coins, and bring the hand out. (*We'll make it a better trick.*)

Tilt the top of the purse toward you and snap it open with the right thumb and first finger. Allow the classic-palmed penny to drop to fingertip rest at that moment as the other right fingers curl naturally. Extend all right fingers and dip them into the open purse, loading the penny inside. Immediately pull the penny out and display both sides. (*I don't know if I told you this, but these pennies aren't worth anything.*) Turn the right hand palm down and lower the penny back into the purse, placing it on the inner side of the two copper/silver coins (which rest copper side outward in the purse). As the right fingers descend into the purse, the first and second fingertips **clip the two copper/silver coins between them**, leaving the genuine penny inside. Bring out the copper/silver coins -- it appears as if you have merely taken the copper coins out one at a time. Snap the purse shut with the left hand and toss it on the table.

Begin curling the right thumb and fingers, at the same time turning the hand palm up, **keeping the copper sides of both coins toward the audience**. They are eventually allowed to fall flatly onto the first and second fingers when the hand is completely palm up. Display the coins (copper side upward) for a moment, then begin to turn the right hand palm down, curling the fingers slightly. **Curl the third finger inward more than the others**, so the two coins flop over, silver sides upward, onto it -- this is hidden from the audience by the backs of the curled first and second fingers. Immediately move the right thumb onto the silver sides and press them flatly against the fingers, which straighten to allow this, as the hand turns palm down. The thumb rapidly shoves both coins, copper sides showing, to the fingertips.

Transfer the coins to between the left thumb, first, and second fingers. (*It would be better if I could change these into coins that are worth something.*) Gesture forward with the right hand. Retract the right hand, turning it palm toward you and moving it in front of the left hand for just a second. At that instant, the left second finger moves to the inner side of the coins, clipping them between it and the first finger, and the thumb moves away -- the coins rotate inward to a horizontal position, copper sides upward. The right thumb immediately descends onto the upper coin. **Push downward with the right thumb while pulling the left thumb out of the way**, rotating the coins farther until their silver sides are toward the audience. Take one between the thumb and fingers of each hand as the hands separate. The change is instantaneous.

After a moment, add the silver coin held by the right hand to the one already in the left hand. Both coins are held in position for the **Take Two Switch**. Gesture palm up with the right hand. (*It would be even better if the coins in your hands changed as well. Take a look.*) During the patter, and while the spectator is occupied, execute the **Take Two Switch** again: the left hand pushes both copper/silver coins into right-hand classic palm, then the right thumb drags one of the finger-palmed gold coins to the right, taking it behind the right fingers. The left hand is also held palm down, the left thumb having pushed the gold coin in that hand to behind the fingers.

The spectator opens his hands and finds two gold coins. (*Two gold coins...which is exactly what I have.*) Look at your hands and turn them partially palm up, displaying the gold coins resting on the tips of the extended second fingers. The classic-palmed coins in the right hand are hidden using the **Kaps Subtlety**: the back of the thumbnail nestles against the inner side of the second finger, the right thumb and thumb base completely concealing the classic-palmed coins from the audience.

Turn the hands palm down and drop one gold coin onto each of his hands. After a slight pause, pick up the purse with the right hand, pulling it over the classic-palmed coins. Tilt the top toward you and snap it open. Drop the gold coins into it two at a time, then snap it shut. As you momentarily allow your hands to relax, falling to your sides, let the classic-palmed coins tumble to finger palm (the purse covers this). Turn the right hand palm up and, using the **Purse Swindle** (page 396 of *The New Modern Coin Magic*, Bobo, 1966), toss the purse to the left hand -- the coins travel conveniently beneath it as it flies through the air. The left hand puts everything back in the pocket to conclude.

## Key Notes
- The misdirection for the Take Two Switch relies on the spectator's momentary confusion and embarrassment at having misinterpreted "palm up" vs. "palm down" -- this psychological moment is the linchpin of the entire opening sequence
- The copper-to-silver change of the two coins (third finger flop + thumb-lift spin) must be practiced until it is a single fluid motion; the spinning action as they rotate from copper to silver side is what sells the magic
- The Kaps Subtlety for displaying gold coins while hiding classic-palmed coins is critical for the clean ending: the thumb base and thumbnail nestling against the second finger create the angle-proof concealment
- The Purse Swindle throw to the left hand is a utility move from Bobo that neatly transports the remaining classic-palmed coins under cover of the purse
- The progressive structure ("good, better, best") gives the routine a built-in theatrical arc that builds logically to the gold coin climax

## Credit
- Malini / Fred Kaps, the "Kaps Subtlety" for concealing classic-palmed coins during open-hand display
- J.B. Bobo, Purse Swindle (*The New Modern Coin Magic*, 1966, page 396)

# Special Delivery
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
An envelope is handed to an impartial observer for safekeeping. A spectator chooses a card and signs it. The signed card is lost in the deck, which is then dropped onto the table. The observer is given a small pocket knife to open the envelope. When he does, he finds another sealed envelope inside. The performer takes the inner envelope, opens its flap, and slides out a third envelope. That envelope is opened to reveal three cards: the first tells the suit of the chosen card, the second reveals its value, and the third card is the signed selection itself. Throughout, the performer's hands are seen unmistakably empty.

## Set-up
- A small pocket knife
- Two extra cards (any Heart card and a black Seven, assuming the force card is the Seven of Hearts — two cards that reflect the suit and value of the force card)
- Three envelopes of nesting sizes: legal/business size (largest), airmail overseas size (middle), regular letter size (smallest)
- An indelible marker
- Place the extra Heart card on top of the black Seven (both face down), insert them into the letter-size envelope and close the flap. Insert this (flap side down) into the airmail overseas envelope (flap side up), close it, turn flap side down. Insert into legal-size envelope (flap side up), lick and seal. Place it in a convenient pocket.
- Place the pocket knife in a left-side coat or loose trousers pocket.
- Have the Seven of Hearts on top of the deck (or bottom-palmed if you let the spectator shuffle).

## Method and Presentation
*(Do you believe in destiny? That seemingly random actions are not random at all but sometimes guided by unseen forces, by the hand of fate...)* Bring out the sealed nest of envelopes and hand it to a spectator on your left. *(I have an envelope here that contains this man's destiny. Not your ultimate destiny, sir, just the outcome of actions you may carry out in a few moments.)*

Force the Seven of Hearts (Classic Force or Slip Force) on the spectator. Have him sign the face with the marker. Insert the card into the deck and secretly control it to the top. **Prepare for the Top to Bottom Pivot Palm** by pulling the deck slightly forward in your left hand. *(Now at this point you may be expecting me to try and find the card...but I'm not going to...it isn't necessary.)* **Execute the palm during the last three words.** Turn slightly to the right, lift the deck with your right hand and drop it onto the table. Rapidly turn to the spectator on the left: *(You're holding that envelope, and I have a knife with which you can open it.)* Slip your left hand into your side pocket, **leave the palmed selection**, and bring out the pocket knife. Open it, allowing your hands to be seen unquestionably empty, and hand it to the spectator.

*(If you would, dramatically, yet carefully, slit the top of that envelope open.)* Take back the knife and say, *(Please take out what's inside and hold it up high so everyone can see.)* Close the knife with your left hand and put it back in the pocket containing the signed selection. **Palm the selection, face toward palm,** while your hand is still in the pocket. Bring your hand out as you ask for the inner envelope, taking it with your right hand: *(Another envelope...the suspense builds.)*

Lower the envelope flap side upward and to the right into your left hand. Your right first and second fingertips clip the outer right corner of the hidden card. Your left first finger pushes inward, **pivoting the card slightly counter-clockwise until it is parallel with the envelope.**

*(And inside this envelope is another envelope.)* Open the flap and slide out the letter-size envelope with your right hand. Once it clears, the flap will snap closed. Rest the letter-size envelope on top of the outer envelope. Turn your right hand palm down and grasp the letter-size envelope at the center of its right long side, thumb beneath and fingers above. Lift it and turn it over, flap side upward, letting it brush across the edge of the overseas envelope. At that instant, **your left fingers shove the concealed card beneath the letter-size envelope, where it is nipped by your right fingertips.** Pull the overseas envelope to the left and drop it to the table (inspired by Jerry Andrus's card and coin techniques).

Return your left hand to the letter-size envelope, palm up in dealing position. Your left fingers press the concealed card against the envelope, freeing your right hand to move a few inches right, turn palm up, and slap the envelope with the backs of the fingers. *(And inside this last little envelope are three cards...)* **Steal the card into right-hand Longitudinal Palm** by turning the right hand palm down and curling fingers onto the card. Use both hands to flip the envelope end for end. Open the flap, dip fingers inside, and slide out the three cards. **Add the palmed card behind them** as you spread them. Drop the envelope.

*(...which will tell me something about your card.)* Turn over the top card: *(The top card is the [suit] of Hearts. There's a one in four chance you selected a Heart. Was your card by any chance a Heart?)* Place it face up on the bottom, spread left. Turn over the next card: *(The next card is the Seven of [Spades or Clubs]. A one in thirteen chance you picked a Seven. Was your card by any chance a Seven?)* Replace it on the face, spread right. A face-down card is seen in the middle. *(So the first card told me that it was a Heart and the second card told me that it was a Seven. Do you know what this last card tells me? How you sign your name.)* Turn it over to reveal the signed selection.

## Key Notes
- The Top to Bottom Pivot Palm (described in "Wishful Thinking") is executed by pulling the deck forward, with the right thumb pushing diagonally inward and left to pivot the top card into palm. The movement is not isolated — it occurs as the right hand descends back onto the deck.
- The card loading onto the envelope uses Jerry Andrus-inspired techniques: the concealed card is pivoted parallel to the envelope, then shuttled beneath the inner envelope during the turn-over action.
- The Longitudinal Palm steal from beneath the envelope: press the base of right fingers at the crease against the envelope edge beside the concealed card, curl fingers onto the card, turn palm down while maintaining light upward pressure, pulling the card into Angle Palm.
- If you have smaller hands, an alternative technique leaves the concealed card at almost right angles to the envelope and steals it directly into Angle Palm without rotating.
- The routine builds through three increasingly impossible revelations: suit prediction, value prediction, and finally the signed card itself.

## Credit
Inspired by Charlie Miller's "Dunbury Delusion." The card-and-envelope loading technique is inspired by Jerry Andrus. The alternative handling borrows from Richard Kaufman's "Envelope" in *CardMagic* (1979).

# Orange
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
A large piece of heavy paper is shown thoroughly on both sides and formed into a cone. A red tomato and a yellow lemon are dropped into the cone. The cone is swirled around and, when tipped over, the result of this magical hybrid rolls out — an orange. It is a visual pun in the spirit of Dr. Seuss: red plus yellow equals orange.

## Set-up
- A piece of heavy yet flexible cardboard, about the size of a large magazine cover
- An orange (can be rather large)
- A small ripe tomato with a healthy red glow
- A small lemon (small red and yellow balls can be substituted but weaken the visual pun)
- You must be wearing a sport jacket and standing. This is not a close-up effect.
- The tomato and lemon begin in your right side jacket pocket.
- The orange and the paper are on a nearby table behind something, or in a case.

## Method and Presentation
Turn to wherever the orange and paper are located. Grasp the orange in your right hand, the paper held over it by your right thumb. Face forward and rapidly move your hands toward each other, openly transferring the paper from hand to hand while **secretly tossing the orange to your left hand**. Position the paper over the orange so it drapes downward and conceals it. (This is a sequence from Jerry Andrus's "Mylar Mystery.")

Use the back of your palm-up right hand to smack the paper. Your right hand immediately grasps the paper at the "smacked" spot, fingers on front and thumb behind, directly below your left hand by about a foot. **Your left hand relaxes and drops the orange into your waiting right fingers** — concealed from the audience by the paper. The moment the orange is airborne, your left hand lets go of the paper, allowing it to pivot beneath your right thumb and flop downward. Your left hand now smacks the paper with the backs of the fingers, then immediately grasps the lowermost corner and wraps it inward around your right hand and the orange, **forming a cone**.

Once the cone is secure, grasp it tightly at the bottom with your left hand. *(A red tomato.)* Your right hand goes into your pocket and brings out the tomato. Drop the tomato into the cone. Tip the cone over and spill the tomato out onto your right hand (your left hand firmly grasps the orange through the paper). Rotate the cone open end upward. *(Red is the important thing to remember here.)* **Pretend to dump the tomato into the cone** by dunking your right hand downward and rolling the tomato down into the hand with your thumb. As your right hand moves away, your left hand jiggles the cone so the supposed tomato (actually the orange) is heard bouncing. Your right hand immediately goes to your right jacket pocket and picks up the lemon, **dropping the tomato in the process**.

Bring out the lemon and place it into the cone. *(And, a yellow lemon.)* Reach inside with your right hand and bring the lemon back into view. *(Yellow being the important thing to remember.)* **Repeat the fake dump action** — apparently dumping the lemon into the cone while retaining it palmed. Your right hand immediately moves to the bottom of the cone and grasps it, taking it from your left hand (the right-hand grip conceals the palmed lemon).

*(I mix the red and yellow and we get...)* Swirl the cone around a few times as if mixing the contents, then tip it to the left so the orange rolls out onto your left palm. Toss the orange to a spectator.

To clean up: relax your right hand's grip and the cone will unfurl and flop forward over the fist, your right thumb pressing the center of its side against the hand. Your left hand smacks the paper with the backs of the fingers, and **you repeat the Andrus transfer sequence, secretly transferring the lemon to your left hand** so your right hand can smack the paper. Place both paper and concealed lemon aside. If wearing a topit on the left side, you could toss the lemon into it.

## Key Notes
- The Andrus "Mylar Mystery" transfer is the core technique: the paper acts as a shield while objects are secretly passed from hand to hand. The smacking and grasping actions provide natural cover.
- Timing is critical: when the orange drops from left hand to right, the left hand must immediately release the paper so it pivots and flops down, masking the transfer.
- The fake dumps (tomato and lemon) rely on the left hand jiggling the cone so the orange inside makes noise, selling the illusion that the named item is inside.
- This is a perfect lead-in to a bill or card to orange routine.

## Credit
Core technique from Jerry Andrus's "Mylar Mystery" (*Sleightly Slanted*, Andrus, 1974).

# Name It!
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
The performer openly writes a prediction on the back of a business card and hands it to a spectator for safekeeping. A second spectator is allowed to select a card freely. The prediction is revealed to match the selection exactly. As a stunning climax, when the chosen card is turned over, the spectator's own first name is discovered written boldly across the back — even though the performer never had access to the card after it was chosen.

## Set-up
- Write the name of a card (e.g., "Two of Clubs") on the back of one of your business cards with an indelible black marker. Make sure the writing does not bleed through.
- Place this pre-marked card on top of a stack of ordinary blank business cards. The stack goes into your right side jacket pocket.
- Place the marker in your inner left breast pocket.
- Use an ordinary deck. Casually locate and control the Two of Clubs to the top before beginning.
- This should not be the first trick you perform. During earlier tricks, casually learn the names of one or two spectators and remember who is who.

## Method and Presentation
Shuffle the deck, retaining the Two of Clubs on top, and hold it face down in left-hand dealing position. Your right hand brings out the stack of business cards, printed side upward. Casually flash both sides of the stack as you talk — the audience sees the blank back of the bottommost card. Your left thumb **peels the top (secretly pre-marked) card onto the deck**. Replace the balance of the stack in your pocket.

Reach into your left breast pocket and bring out the marker. Hold the marker deep in your hand. Use your right thumb and first finger to **lift the business card by its inner end and lever it to a vertical position**, printed side directly toward the audience. Rotate the top of the deck toward you so the back of your hand faces the audience. Lay the business card, pre-marked side toward you, flatly against the deck. Your left second, third, and little fingertips hold it in place.

Loosen the cap of the marker, then slip it between your left first and second fingers. Rotate your left hand so the left long side of the deck is upward, pulling downward on the business card with your left fingertips to **hinge it open, exposing the back of the top card**. Your right hand hovers at the right end during this to conceal the business card's new position from spectators on the right. *(I'm going to make a prediction.)* **Pretend to write the name of a card, but actually write the name of one of the spectators on the back of the top card of the deck.** *(A prediction of things to come. Sometimes they don't come, but that's a chance we all take, isn't it?)* Pause to let the ink dry, then squeeze the business card back in position, curling your left fingers tightly. Cap the marker and return it to your breast pocket.

Lean to the right and angle the deck to show your "prediction" to a spectator on that side — the business card covers the spectator's name written on the top of the deck. Turn your left hand palm down and **slide the business card out from under the deck with your right hand**, printed side upward. Place it on the table and ask that spectator to cover it with his hand.

While your left hand is still palm down, grasp the end of the face-up deck with your right hand. Return the deck to left-hand dealing position, now face up. The Two of Clubs, with the spectator's name written on its back, is at the rear. **Control the Deuce into forcing position tenth from the face** (Gary uses a reverse Hofzinser Spread Cull counting nine cards, then slipping the Two of Clubs beneath them; alternatively, count ten cards from the bottom, hold a pinky break, and Double Cut to the break).

*(I've actually written the name of a card that's presently lost somewhere in this deck.)* Turn to the spectator guarding the prediction: *(That's true, isn't it?)* He will say yes.

Begin stud dealing cards to the table face down one at a time. *(I am going to deal cards on the table one at a time [deal first, second, third], as I do [deal fourth, fifth] I'd like you to just [deal sixth, seventh] call stop [deal eighth].)* **The spectator will almost invariably stop you on the tenth card.** If he does not, begin a very lazy necktie Second Deal — Gary starts tilting the deck face up toward him very slowly around the seventh card just in case.

When stopped, spread the face-down pile to the left, then deal the face-up Two of Clubs onto it, outjogged. Turn the rest of the deck face down and lower it onto the other face-down cards. The Two of Clubs remains outjogged and visible. *(You could have stopped me at any one of these other cards, or even any of these. But you stopped at the Two of Clubs, which is in fact the very same prediction I made.)* Dramatically pick up the business card and turn it over to reveal the prediction.

After a pause, say, *(You may be wondering how I knew you were going to stop at the Two of Clubs. It's because people have a very strong affinity with their names. And this Two of Clubs has your name on the back.)* Turn over the Two of Clubs to reveal the spectator's name. They are left with two souvenirs.

## Key Notes
- The psychological force relies on the timing of the patter. Most people build up anxiety while you deal and talk, waiting for you to stop talking before saying "stop." The eighth card is dealt just as you finish the instruction to "call stop."
- The pre-marked business card serves as a shield while you actually write the spectator's name on the back of the top card. The hinging action with the card tucked against the deck is the crucial subtlety.
- The business card stack flash: you show the blank back of the bottom card, not the pre-marked top card, when casually flashing both sides.
- For walkaround repeat performances, prepare all business cards with descending values (8, 7, 6, 5...) in Clubs-Hearts-Spades-Diamonds order, or match your preferred stack order (Si Stebbins, Eight Kings, etc.).

## Credit
The psychological force concept and name-on-card climax are Gary Kurtz originals. The method of secretly writing on a card beneath a business card shield is Kurtz's own technique.

# The Pocket Game
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
The performer states that he will remove a coin from his pocket first, concealing its identity until the spectator does likewise. Whatever coin the spectator removes, the performer will have removed that identical coin a moment earlier. The spectator and performer each take a pocket containing a nickel, penny, dime, and quarter. All four attempts succeed perfectly — each time the performer anticipates the spectator's choice.

## Set-up
- Three complete sets of nickels, dimes, quarters, and pennies — twelve coins total.
- All twelve coins begin in an otherwise empty left trousers or jacket pocket.

## Method and Presentation
*(There are certain people, like chess players and medalists, who have developed an uncanny ability to anticipate their opponents' moves. I've developed a game of anticipation of my own which I'd like to show you. Do you have an empty pocket?)*

Reach into the pocket with your left hand, bring all twelve coins out, and dump them onto your right palm openly and visibly.

*(I've got some small change here of which I'd like to give you a penny, nickel, dime, and quarter.)* Tilt your right hand slightly toward you so your palm is no longer visible to the audience. Using your left thumb and first finger:

- **Pick up two dimes.** Insert one into a clipped position at the juncture of your right third and little fingers. Hand the other dime to the spectator.
- **Pick up two pennies.** Clip one at the juncture of your right second and third fingers. Give the other penny to the spectator.
- **Pick up both nickels.** Clip one at the juncture of your right first and second fingers. Hand the other nickel to the spectator.
- **Pick up both quarters.** Shove one into thumb palm. Hand the other quarter to the spectator.

*(Place them into your pocket and I'll do the same.)* Turn your right hand palm down over your palm-up left hand, dumping the four loose coins (one of each denomination) onto your left palm. A brief flash of these coins is allowed. Place them into the left-side pocket from which you originally took them.

*(The idea is this: I'm going to remove one coin from my pocket; and then you're going to remove one coin from your pocket. The idea is for me to take out the same coin that you do, but before you do.)* Show your left hand empty, then reach into the pocket and pretend to remove one coin. **Actually, your left fist comes out empty.** The spectator follows suit and randomly removes one coin from his pocket.

When he reveals his coin, **execute the L'Homme Masque Load with the matching denomination.** Rotate your left fist so the thumb hole is upward. Point to his hand with your palm-down right hand: *(You took the penny from your pocket.)* Your right hand passes over your left fist in a gesture and **releases the matching coin by raising the appropriate finger — the coin drops into the left thumb hole.** Immediately turn your left fist palm upward, drawing your right hand inward and pointing to your pocket: *(The one I took from my pocket was also a penny.)* Open your left hand to reveal the coin and drop it on the table.

The misdirection for the load: first look at the coin in his hand, then up at him while the load takes place and you gesture toward your pocket, then down at your left hand as you open it.

**Repeat the handling with a second coin.** Left hand goes into the pocket and comes out empty in a fist. Load the matching coin via L'Homme Masque Load.

You are now left with two coins concealed in your right hand. Bring your hands together in a natural clasped position (right over left): *(I have to admit I've played this game before.)* In this position, **allow one of the remaining coins to drop into finger palm in your left hand**, noting its denomination. Separate your hands, left hand turning palm down and going into the pocket. This time it closes into a fist and comes out with the finger-palmed coin.

Ask the spectator to bring out another coin. If he brings out the matching denomination, simply open your left hand to display it. If he brings out the other denomination, **execute a Roth Shuttle Pass**: your left hand turns palm down, apparently dumping its coin into your right hand (which turns palm up to catch it), while the coin is retained in left-hand finger palm and the correct coin is displayed on your right fingers.

For the fourth and final coin: either it is already in your left hand (if the spectator chose the quarter at the last step) — simply pretend to bring it from the pocket. Or it is in your right hand — insert your left hand into the pocket as if bringing out the last coin (actually empty), then your right hand pretends to pick it up, pushing the palmed coin to the fingertips. *(And for the last one I'll choose a quarter, because I knew that would be the coin you left in your pocket until the end.)*

## Key Notes
- You must remember exactly which coin is clipped where: dime (third/little fingers), penny (second/third fingers), nickel (first/second fingers), quarter (thumb palm). This memory is critical for the L'Homme Masque Load to work instantly.
- The initial loading of coins into clipped positions is done openly in front of the audience under the guise of simply separating the coins — this is what makes the trick practical.
- The L'Homme Masque Load misdirection is subtle but powerful: eye contact naturally draws attention away from the hands at the critical moment.
- For the final two coins, the Roth Shuttle Pass provides a clean switch if the spectator's choice does not match what you hold.
- This routine works because the spectator genuinely has free choice of all four coins — yet you are always one step ahead.

## Credit
Based on handlings by Tommy Dowd (*The Tarbell Course in Magic*, 1943) and Ed Marlo (*Amazing, Isn't It?*, 1942), with coin techniques from standard sources. The L'Homme Masque Load application is Kurtz's.

# The Empty Hand
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
After a card has been selected and palmed from the deck, the performer pulls his jacket open with his right hand to show the empty interior pocket. He swings the jacket closed, and in that same motion his left hand darts inside. The left hand immediately emerges from behind the jacket at breast pocket level, holding the selected card. The hands are seen unmistakably empty both before and after the production, creating a powerful psychological impression that the card has materialized from nowhere inside the jacket.

## Set-up
- A card must already be selected, controlled, and palmed in the right hand.
- The deck is placed aside.
- The performer must be wearing a sport jacket.

## Method and Presentation
Your right hand grasps the spot on the jacket just beneath the bottom of the lapel between thumb and fingertips, palm parallel with the jacket. Pull the right side of the jacket open, allowing the audience to see the empty pocket inside. *(Look at the pocket and gesture with your obviously empty left hand.)*

As your right hand swings the jacket closed, **bend it both inward and downward at the wrist until it is practically at right angles to the jacket.** Your second fingertip curls inward and moves onto the back of the palmed card. By this time the jacket is almost closed and the back of your hand is toward the audience. **Swiftly straighten your second finger, propelling the card out of your hand and inside the jacket.** Once the second finger has straightened as much as possible, **your third finger, which has curled in readiness, takes over and pushes the card diagonally upward.**

Simultaneously, your left hand enters the right side of the jacket at pocket level. Once out of sight, **your left hand bends downward at the wrist and grasps the card.** Your left wrist then straightens and arches, raising the card to the level of the pocket and a bit higher, bringing it out from inside the jacket almost at collarbone level and into audience view.

## Key Notes
- The manipulation around the edge of the jacket sounds difficult but becomes easy once you coordinate the bending of the right wrist and the pushing of the fingers while swinging the jacket closed.
- The entire action takes a split second.
- This handling provides a stronger psychological impression than the Vernon method because throwing the jacket open gives the absolute impression that both hands are empty.
- The technique is applied in more complex form during "Interchange," where two cards must be manipulated simultaneously.

## Credit
Gary Kurtz. A variation on Dai Vernon's card-to-pocket technique from *Further Inner Secrets of Card Magic*, which itself was fashioned from handlings by Tommy Dowd (*The Tarbell Course in Magic*, 1943) and Ed Marlo (*Amazing, Isn't It?*, 1942).

# Interchange
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
Two spectators each name a four-of-a-kind (one face cards, one spot cards), which are openly removed from the deck and each card is initialed. The eight cards are shown -- all four Queens and all four Deuces. The performer places the four Deuces one at a time into four different pockets (right side, left side, inner right breast, inner left breast). He announces that the Queens will change places with the Deuces. One by one, the Queens held in the hand transform into Deuces. The performer then reaches into each pocket and produces the four Queens, showing that the transposition has occurred across four separate locations.

## Set-up
- An ordinary deck of cards.
- Two markers for the spectators to initial cards.
- Performer must wear a sport jacket with four accessible pockets (right side, left side, inner right breast, inner left breast).
- No gimmicks or duplicates required.

## Method and Presentation
*(I'd like to show you something special, something I don't do for just anyone.)* Ask two spectators to each name a four-of-a-kind. Remove those eight cards from the deck and have each spectator initial their four cards. Place the face cards behind the spot cards, both groups in alternating red/black order.

To show the Queens: Square the packet face down. **Spread off cards one at a time, flashing each Queen's face**, but secretly **let the two cards below a right thumbtip break drop back onto the left-hand cards** after the third Queen is taken. The right hand immediately flashes the fourth Queen a second time, giving the impression all four were seen. Table the right-hand packet (actually only two Queens).

For the Deuces: Turn the left-hand packet (two Deuces with two Queens hidden behind) face up and **execute the Las Vegas Ascanio Spread**, taking the final triple card (two Queens hidden behind the displayed Deuce) with the right hand in Straddle Grip. Separate hands, each apparently holding two Deuces, and **wiggle the uppermost cards to emphasize their singularity**. Return cards and flip packet face down, obtaining a break beneath the triple card.

**Perform a Triple Lift** to display the Two of Clubs, name it aloud, turn it face down, and thumb off the top (actually a Queen) into the right side jacket pocket. Repeat with **another Triple Lift** for the Two of Hearts into the left side jacket pocket.

Do another **Triple Lift** to show the Two of Spades face up. **Your left fingers immediately flip over the remaining single card** to display the Two of Diamonds. Lower the Diamond onto the triple card and square. Your left hand slides the Two of Diamonds off the face ostensibly toward the inner right breast pocket. As the jacket opens, **your right hand hooks third and pinky fingertips around the lapel edge**. Your left hand guides the card behind the jacket and **bends downward at the wrist, guiding the card beneath the tips of your right third and little fingers**, which curl inward pressing the card against the cloth. Left hand emerges empty and pats the pocket. **Right third and little fingers realign the card with the packet** using a reverse Kelly Bottom Placement.

Your right hand takes the remaining packet to the inner left breast pocket. Out of sight, **swing the four-card packet into Angle Palm** and press the inner long side against your chest. Spread your right thumb and fingers far apart while tapping the pocket top (Vernon's technique from *The Dai Vernon Book of Magic*). Regrip into Angle Palm. *(That's four Deuces in four different pockets.)*

Pick up the tabled Queens. Your right hand adds the **Angle Palmed cards on top** while revolving the Queen packet face down end over end. Your left hand tugs up your right sleeve as cover. *(The Queens are going to change places with the Deuces...)*

**Execute an Elmsley Count** to display three Queens and the Two of Diamonds among them. Outjog the Deuce. Make a magical gesture toward a different pocket, then **Buckle to display more Deuces** using the Las Vegas Ascanio Spread. Repeat with the third pocket via another Buckle. For the fourth pocket, **use Zarrow's Triple Buckle** (Gary buckles all three cards with the first finger) to reveal all four Deuces.

**Reverse count** the packet, then **Double Buckle to obtain a break above the two lowermost Queens**. Your left third finger curls beneath and clips the Queens. Straightening your fingers shoves the Queens beneath your right hand into full palm. Separate the hands to display Deuces only. *(These four Deuces used to be in four different pockets. This can only mean that the four Queens are now in those pockets.)*

Now apply "The Empty Hand" technique with two cards: your right hand grasps the lapel, pulls the jacket open, then swings it closed. **Your right second and third fingers rapidly shove the two palmed Queens around the edge of the jacket.** Your left hand darts inside, bends at the wrist, and grasps one Queen. **The left hand straightens and pulls that Queen out at breast pocket level.** Use Mario's Search technique: left fingers grasp the left lapel to pull it open slightly while the right hand, holding the second Queen pressed inside the jacket, moves smoothly across to beneath the left side. **Mime pulling the card from the left breast pocket** and produce the second Queen. Produce the third Queen from the right side pocket and the fourth from the left side pocket, displaying all four fanned.

## Key Notes
- The alternating red/black order is important for the visual display of the cards.
- The routine is designed for standup use in front of larger groups.
- This version eliminates an unfortunate cleanup moment that occurs at the wrong time in earlier methods.
- The application of "The Empty Hand" with two cards is more challenging since two cards are being moved, and the right third-finger action is partially lost.
- The Las Vegas Ascanio Spread and Straddle Grip are essential techniques for this routine.
- Every time the packet returns to left-hand dealing position, the triple card should be conveniently injogged for the next phase.

## Credit
Gary Kurtz. Based on Bro. John Hamman's "The Underground Transposition" (*The Secrets of Brother John Hamman*, Kaufman, 1989) and Jerry Sadowitz's "The More Things Change" (*Alternative Card Magic*, 1982). Incorporates Dai Vernon's "The Travelers" concept.

# Modern Art
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
The performer discusses the absurdity of modern art while shuffling a deck. A spectator stops at a randomly chosen card -- the Four of Spades. The performer signs his name on the face (as a famous artist would) and has the spectator sign the back. With a dramatic brush of the fingers, the card instantly gains yellow and blue coloring, becoming "Richard's Choice in Yellow and Blue." The performer then announces a new piece: by mixing yellow and blue, he creates green. He turns the card over, removes it, and tears it cleanly in half along a central unmarked strip. Pressing the halves together face to face and wriggling them, he peels them apart to reveal they have become entirely green -- "Richard's Choice Torn in Green."

## Set-up
- Two duplicate Four of Spades prepared in advance:
  - One with a large green rectangle drawn on the face, leaving an unmarked strip down the center.
  - One with two squares drawn on the face -- one blue, one yellow -- leaving an unmarked strip between them.
  - Both cards have the performer's name printed in identical block letters.
- An ordinary deck containing its own Four of Spades.
- A black marker.
- **Stack from the top down:** face-down unmarked Four of Spades, face-up blue/yellow Four, face-up green Four, rest of deck face down.

## Method and Presentation
*(Do you like modern art? I think it's a bit much sometimes...)* False shuffle the deck retaining the top stack. **Force the Four of Spades** using either a face-up Dribble Force (after secretly shuttling the unmarked Four beneath the face-up deck via reverse Hofzinser Spread Cull) or a Classic Force. Take the chosen Four and slip it face down beneath the deck while flipping the deck face up -- all three Fours are now face up on top of the deck.

*(If I were a famous artist all I would have to do is sign my name on this card and it would be instant "art.")* Sign your name on the face card in identical block letters. Hand the marker to the spectator and have him sign the back. **Do a Triple Turnover**, flipping all three Fours face down as one so he can sign.

**Do a second Triple Turnover** to flip all three face up again. *(But when you think of art you think of color...)* **Execute the Erdnase Second Transformation** (with Howard Schwarzman's finesse): your right little fingertip contacts the outer end of the face card and, in straightening the fingers, shoves it forward slightly so the heel of your hand can contact the second card from the face. Slide your hand inward, dragging the second card with it. When the outer end of the second card clears the inner end of the outjogged face card, reverse direction -- slide the yellow/blue Four over the unmarked Four while left fingers square from beneath. **Slowly brush your right fingertips back and forth over the card to dramatically "paint" the colors on.** *(Richard's Choice in Yellow and Blue.)*

*(And now I will mix the yellow and blue and create a piece titled "Richard's Choice Torn in Green.")* **Do a Triple Turnover** to flip the three Fours face down. Remove the top card (the green Four) and place the deck aside. **Holding the face of the green Four toward you, tear it cleanly in half along the uncolored line running down the middle.** Without flashing the faces, **press the halves together face to face and wriggle them against one another as if mixing yellow with blue.** After a few moments, **peel the halves apart and turn them around to reveal the new green color on both halves.**

## Key Notes
- The signatures on the prepared cards must look identical in both appearance and position to the one signed during performance.
- When tearing, keep the faces toward yourself so the audience never sees that the card was already green on one side.
- The strip down the center of the prepared cards is intentionally unmarked to provide a natural and convincing tear line.
- This trick works best for hip or art-savvy audiences. The humor transcends the specific theme.

## Credit
Gary Kurtz. The Erdnase Second Transformation is from *The Expert at the Card Table* (Andrews, 1902). The Howard Schwarzman finesse for that move is credited within.

# Flurious
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
This is a standup or close-up coin routine in five seamless parts. Part One: Three silver dollars appear one at a time from an empty hand, seemingly multiplying with each magical gesture. Part Two: All three coins vanish at once, then silently reappear. Part Three: The coins vanish and are reproduced again one by one, returning to the performer's "former affluence." Part Four: The coins are displayed in a fan, the shell is secretly extracted, and each coin appears to travel invisibly from the right hand to the left, one after another, with poetic patter. Part Five: Using Roth's "Hanging Coins" technique, the coins vanish one at a time in reverse order, ultimately leaving both hands empty -- "(...a memory, misty, like a dream.)"

## Set-up
- Three silver dollars (or half dollars), all "soft" (worn smooth for noiseless handling).
- One matching shell, equally worn.
- **Nest the shell over one coin** so the shelled coin is in the center of the stack.
- Place the stack into the right front trousers pocket with the shell mouth facing away from you.
- No other gimmicks required.

## Method and Presentation

### Part One: The Appearances
*(I'm going to begin by showing you an illusion... I'm talking about money.)* During the opening, your right hand picks up the stack inside the pocket and transfers it to **Roth's Curl Palm**: finger palm the stack, curl fingers until perpendicular, lower thumb onto it, roll upward slightly, wrap second finger around the bottom and pull outward while pushing down with thumb to lever the stack into Curl Palm inside the curled second finger. The shell is now mouth upward.

Pull up both sleeves to show hands empty. Pluck an invisible coin from the air with the left hand. *(I will create, from nothingness, my own.)* Transfer it to the right hand. Your second finger pulls the Curl Palmed stack forward, transferring it to **Edge Grip**.

**Edge Grip Squeeze Production**: Bring the left hand palm toward audience over the right hand, left thumb beneath right second finger. Curl and spread right fingers into Edge Grip Display. Rapidly rotate the left hand until fingers curl beneath the stack. **Allow one coin to drop off the bottom** onto left fingers. Curl left fingers to turn the coin over and deliver its right edge between right thumb and second finger. Reveal in Edge Grip Display. *(That's an 1886 Morgan silver dollar... an illusion of quality.)*

For the second coin: Let the visible coin tumble onto the left hand. Replace it, but the coin now lines up in left-hand finger palm. **Left thumb presses on top of the stack in Edge Grip while right third finger presses upward against the bottom.** Curl left fingers to complete the finger palm, then straighten all fingers -- the stack turns flatly against fingertips. **Separate hands, taking a coin in each hand** (the shelled coin in the right, shell side toward audience). *(...money begets money, which in turn begets money.)*

For the third coin: Push the right-hand coin to thumb and first fingertip. Return the left-hand coin to the right hand behind the shelled coin, spread downward. Bring hands together, **left thumb shoves the finger-palmed coin directly behind the visible coins, centered between them.** Left first finger presses against the face; left hand swivels so the right thumb can move onto the upper edge and second finger beneath the lower edge. **Squeeze the coins flush, then rapidly fan them with the right hand** -- the third coin has appeared. *(Not a bad way to make a living.)*

### Part Two: The Vanish and Reappearance
Left hand plucks the shelled coin off the front of the fan. Right thumb lets the other two coins fall one at a time onto the shelled coin. *(Three turn-of-the-century pieces of pure silver.)* **Execute Dingle's "Silent Steal"**: left hand turns palm down, fingers closing into a fist as the stack is lowered onto fingertips and pushed out the heel of the hand. Right hand descends palm-down over the left. Once covered, turn left hand palm up so the stack nestles against the right thumb, which bends and grasps them. Retract the right hand, stealing the stack. Left hand crumples and opens to show the vanish. *(Unfortunately they have a tendency to vanish as easily as they came.)*

For the reappearance: Left hand strokes the right palm from left to right, then reverses the arc. As it rounds the thumb crotch, **the stack held by the right thumb falls naturally into left-hand finger palm.** Both hands separate palm down. *(Reality comes rushing in once again to burst the bubble of my new-found financial security.)*

### Part Three: Second Reproduction Sequence
Right hand plucks an invisible coin from the air, focuses attention on it. Meanwhile, left palm turns toward you, second fingertip curls to rest on the edge of the finger-palmed stack. Right hand descends over left as if placing the invisible coin into Spellbound position. **Guide the finger-palmed stack against the right thumb** (version of Dingle's "Silent Steal"). *(Which I make real, which I make two, which I make three...)*

Left hand swoops downward and to the right in a hooking motion, **left fingertips slide the outermost coin to the left** where left thumb completes the grip. The right hand moves to the right revealing the coin. Repeat for the second coin, this time the coin hits the right thumb base and is shoved downward inside the left fingers. For the third coin, move hands together, dipping the two right-hand coins partially behind the left fingertips. **Left thumb pushes the finger-palmed coin upward and to the right until it glides behind the uppermost right-hand coin**, where it is nipped by the right thumbtip. Fan three coins.

### Part Four: The Travels
Allow two coins to topple from the fan onto the left hand, leaving the shelled coin in the right hand. **Tilt the shelled coin so the solid coin drops out** -- the shell remains in right-hand finger palm. *(Up to this point the coins have been appearing and disappearing...)*

Left thumb shoves the upper two coins forward toward fingertips. Hands meet -- left thumb guides the two coins in front of the shell. **Right thumb and fingers take both coins and the shell and fan them**, shell uppermost and innermost. *(I will now make them appear to travel from one place to another.)*

Look at the right hand, then the audience. While attention shifts, **the right thumb pulls the shell downward and inward behind the two visible coins** (third fingertip prevents the visible coins from moving). Left hand produces its finger-palmed coin. *(That's the first coin, having gone from right to left.)*

To set up the second travel: right hand comes over, uses its lowermost coin to tap the coin on left fingers. Draw the right hand inward until its lowermost coin is poised directly over the left pinky base. **Simultaneously turn both hands palm toward you, laying the lower right-hand coin into left-hand finger palm.** Right fingers press the left-hand coin in place. Smoothly separate hands -- right thumb pushes the shell up into view so it appears two coins remain there. *(One down, two to go.)*

For the third travel, repeat the same sleight. The left hand now has the shell in finger palm and two visible coins at the fingertips. For the vanish of the last right-hand coin, **use Latta's Curl Palm vanish**: move thumb onto the right edge, lever to horizontal, curl third finger around the circumference into Curl Palm, extend right thumbtip to second fingertip as if pushing the coin there. Raise hand to lips and blow. Lower hand, bend back at wrist to show empty palm with fingers spread. Left hand produces three coins.

### Part Five: The Hanging Coins Conclusion
*(Let me go back a little further in time, before I had the third coin, when I had only two...)* Using **Roth's "Hanging Coins"** technique: take the rightmost coin, lever it into Edge Grip as the right hand descends over the left palm as if placing the coin there. Left hand closes around the supposed coin. Rotate left hand clockwise, **spread the shelled coin upward with the left thumb**, and take the two visible coins in Edge Grip Display in the right hand. Left hand crumples and opens empty.

Execute **Gary's Gesticulated Changeover**: left hand meets right hand so the lowermost coin enters left-hand finger palm. Right thumb presses the shelled coin against the inside of left fingers. Curl right third fingertip beneath the Edge-Gripped coin, freeing the right thumb to lever it vertical. Separate hands and turn palm down, pushing coins to fingertips.

A feint: right hand pretends to place its coin into the left hand via Edge Grip Placement. Left hand rotates clockwise and places its coin into Edge Grip Display in the right hand. Left hand opens -- the coin is still there (revealing the finger-palmed coin). *(Before I had a second coin.)*

Transfer the shelled coin to beside the left-hand coin. Right hand returns and **takes the shelled coin into Edge Grip** -- left second finger presses inward on the shelled coin, causing it to pivot horizontally around the lower edge of the other coin, and slide beneath the coin already in right-hand Edge Grip.

Pretend to place the coin into the left hand (both coins remain in Edge Grip). Left hand transfers the remaining visible coin to Edge Grip Display in the right hand. Left hand opens empty. *(I had only one.)*

For the final vanish: left hand retakes the visible coin in Spellbound position. Right third fingertip curls beneath the two Edge-Gripped coins. The visible coin falls backward onto left fingers. Right hand turns palm down, the two coins settle to fingertip rest, then elevate into classic palm. Right hand takes the visible coin from the left hand. **Execute a Retention Pass** to vanish it, closing left fingers while the coin is pulled into fingertip rest in the right hand. *(If we went back any further...)*

Mime pushing the coin to left fingertips, then move the right hand toward the left. On the way, **press the coin from fingertip rest upward into classic palm**. Crumple left fingers open to show empty. Turn the invisible coin between hands, toss it upward. Separate hands, raising both palm upward -- the three coins in classic palm rest in the center of the right hand. Slightly curl the first finger and pull downward to create a bump at the finger base, hiding the coins and giving the hand an empty appearance. *(A memory, misty, like a dream.)*

Clean up by inserting the right hand into a pocket, curling the fingers to squeeze the stack onto the fingers and leave it silently.

## Key Notes
- "Soft" coins (worn smooth) are essential -- they slide noiselessly and remain together when dropped in a stack.
- The routine requires mastery of Edge Grip, Curl Palm, classic palm, and finger palm in various combinations.
- Patter delivery is droll and absurd -- the "memory, misty, like a dream" line is delivered with comedic dryness.
- The Roth-inspired handlings of Edge Grip keep the coins horizontal while the empty palm is visible to the audience.
- All finger palm techniques should utilize only the third finger, allowing the second finger to remain partially straightened for a natural look.
- This routine has fooled magicians from coast to coast and is Gary's most often performed coin piece.
- The routine combines elements of John Ramsay's and David Roth's approaches throughout.

## Credit
Gary Kurtz. Heavy influences from John Ramsay and David Roth. Incorporates Derek Dingle's "Silent Steal" (*The Complete Works of Derek Dingle*, Kaufman, 1982), David Roth's "Hanging Coins" (*David Roth's Expert Coin Magic*, Kaufman, 1985), Geoffrey Latta's Curl Palm vanish (previously unpublished), and Roth's Edge Grip techniques. Also references David Williamson's "Wishing Well" (*Williamson's Wonders*, Kaufman, 1989), Al Schneider (in Gary Oullet's *Close-Up Illusions*, 1990), and Slydini-style crossover sequences.

# Collectors (All Together)
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
Four Jacks are shown face up and dropped in a pile on the table. Three spectators each select and remember a card, which are lost in the deck. The deck is placed aside. The Jacks are picked up and spread, and three face-down cards are seen interlaced between them. These are removed one by one and turned over to reveal the three signed selections. There is no extraneous activity -- just a clean, direct picture of the magic.

## Set-up
- An ordinary deck of cards
- Two markers (one for each of two spectators, optional -- for initialing)
- Wear a sport jacket (cards are placed in jacket pockets during routine)
- No gimmicks or duplicate cards required

## Method and Presentation
*(It's a pretty well-accepted fact that people like to collect things. Some people collect stamps or coins. Other slightly more offbeat people might be absorbed in collecting vintage automobiles, or small corporations.)* Spread through a shuffled deck and remove the four Jacks. *(Well, cards are like people, too.)* Maneuver the deck into face-down dealing position, hold the Jacks face up and spread in the right hand. *(Especially the face cards.)* Tilt the top of the deck toward you and **obtain a left pinky break beneath the top card.** Drop the Jacks on top of the deck and begin **Marlo's ATFUS**: the face-up Jacks are squared on top, your right hand **regrips all five cards above the break in Biddle Grip** and lifts them off slightly. Your left thumb **injogs the new top card of the deck about half the width of the white border.** The right hand returns, lowering the Jacks onto the deck. Your right thumbtip **contacts the inner end of the injogged card and lifts it beneath the five-card packet**, maintaining a thumb break. Your left thumb peels the uppermost Jack off onto the deck, then slides it beneath the right-hand packet protruding to the left. Everything is squared against the left thumbtip. Peel off the second Jack the same way. For the third Jack, move the packet back over the deck, then **leave the three cards beneath the right thumbtip break flush on top of the deck.** Peel off the third Jack, slip it beneath the packet, square. The right hand now holds two face-up Jacks with a face-down indifferent card sandwiched between them; the other two Jacks are face-up beneath the face-down top card of the deck. *(These four Jacks, in particular, like to collect things. In particular, they like to collect...other cards.)* Table the right-hand packet.

Spread the face-down deck, shoving over the top five cards as a group so the face-up Jacks are not exposed. Have three cards chosen. Square the deck and **obtain a left pinky break beneath the top three cards** (beneath the lowermost face-up Jack). Right hand grips those three cards lightly from above in Biddle Grip, angling their inner ends slightly to the right. The right hand then grips the entire deck, freeing the left hand to **cut off the lower half and place it on top.** The three angled cards are now in the center, corners hidden beneath the thumbs and fingers. *(This way they'll collect your, and only your, cards.)*

**Secretly shift the angled block to protrude at the outer left corner**, hidden by the left thumbtip. Raise the hands so the face of the deck is toward the audience and **snap it into a wide Pressure Fan.** From the rear you can see both face-up Jacks, but they are not visible to the audience. Insert the first selection into the fan on the far side of the leftmost Jack, upjogged halfway. Insert the second selection between the Jacks, slightly to the left. Insert the third selection above both Jacks, slightly to the right. It appears the three selections are widely separated in the fan.

Close the fan and push the three outjogged selections flush, **angling them slightly to the right** so your left pinky can contact their inner right corners and push upward. Your right hand lifts the upper half and the five angled cards. The left hand **cuts the lower half to the top**, bringing the five angled cards to the bottom. Pull down with the right pinky to obtain a break above them. You are now in position to **Bottom Palm the five-card block.** The left pinky, maintaining pressure, **snaps inward a la Erdnase**, doubling over and pivoting the block into palm (pivot point: inner left corner, nestled in the left thumb base). The instant the block swings into position, your right first finger lifts half the deck to begin a **Swing Cut** -- this hides the finger movement of the palm. *(At this point you might expect me to try and find your cards, but I'm not going to.)* Right hand lifts the deck and **drops it on the table.** Your left hand turns inward so the five palmed cards are not exposed.

Right hand picks up the Jack packet and displays it all around, then places it into the left hand in dealing position. *(I don't have to because the Jacks have already collected them.)* Execute **Bottom Palm Replacement** (as described in Wishful Thinking): left pinky scoots to the inner side of the palmed card, other fingers relax, the curled card springs out flat and rotates into position beneath the packet. Spread the cards between your hands to reveal three face-down cards interlaced among the face-up Jacks.

Hold the last two cards (a face-up Jack with a fourth face-down card beneath it) as one. Outjog the three face-down cards. Reach around the outer end with your right second fingertip to contact the face-down indifferent card on the very bottom (aligned with the Jacks). **Pull that card forward beneath the other outjogged cards** -- it appears as if you have merely pulled out the lowermost outjogged card. Revolve it face up end over end to reveal the first selection. For the second selection, genuinely remove the lowermost outjogged card and revolve it face up.

For the third selection, turn the left hand palm down so the audience can see the face of the third selection jutting out. As the right hand reaches over to withdraw it, **the right first and second fingers secretly push the lowermost outjogged (indifferent) card flush with the Jacks.** Immediately slide out the third selection and drop it on the table. Execute a **Triple Buckle** to snap the Jacks into a face-up four-card spread, with the right hand holding a double card (the face-down indifferent card hidden beneath the uppermost Jack). Reverse count the three loose Jacks onto the double card one at a time, square the packet, and place it on top of the deck to eliminate the indifferent card from play.

## Key Notes
- The directness of this handling is its greatest strength. No unnecessary actions -- the theatrical arc is clean: Jacks shown, selections lost, Jacks found to have collected them.
- The combination of Elmsley's notion with Audley Walsh's Bluff Multiple Shift makes the insertion and control of selections unusually deceptive.
- The Bottom Palm is synchronized with a Swing Cut as cover -- the cut occurs a split second after the pinky snaps the cards into palm, hiding all finger movement.
- During the final revelation, the fake pull-through of the indifferent card (pretending it is one of the outjogged selections) is a subtle but vital piece of misdirection.
- The Triple Buckle at the end uses the same technique as described in Interchange: the left first fingertip presses upward against the outer right corner of the bottom card, buckling it and successive cards with progressive inward bending of the finger.

## Credit
Roy Walton (original Collectors plot), Alex Elmsley (notion for handling the angled block), Audley Walsh (Bluff Multiple Shift), Ed Marlo (ATFUS)

# Cigar
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
A spectator lends a bill, which is signed. The performer rolls the bill into a tube, lights a lighter near it as if to burn it, then instead tilts the rolled bill and produces a cigar from inside it. The cigar is handed to a spectator. The bill is then folded smaller and smaller until it vanishes completely. The spectator is asked to unwrap and break the cigar in half, discovering the folded bill inside. The bill is unfolded and the spectator's signature is verified. At no point does the bill leave the audience's sight during the crucial switch.

## Set-up
- A cigar (six to eight inches) in its cellophane wrapper
- A bill (any denomination) to match the borrowed bill
- An indelible marker
- A cigarette lighter
- A thumbtip
- Prepare the cigar: carefully open one end of the cellophane wrapper and slide the cigar out. Using tweezers, remove half the tobacco from the open end. Neatly fold the bill for the Klause Bill Switch (four folds: in half, in quarters, in eighths, in sixteenths), firmly creasing each fold. Slide the folded bill inside the cigar, repack as much tobacco back as possible until it looks normal. Discard excess tobacco. Re-wrap the cigar in its cellophane and seal the top with a drop of glue.
- Place the prepared cigar and the marker in the inner right breast pocket (shirt or jacket)
- Place the lighter and thumbtip into the right side jacket pocket (if there is a ticket pocket, place the thumbtip in it)

## Method and Presentation
Ask a spectator to lend a bill. While they are busy getting one, **your left hand reaches into the right breast pocket containing the cigar and marker, lifts both, and drops the cigar down the inside of your right sleeve** (keeping the right arm slightly bent at the elbow). Bring out the marker, uncap it, and hand it to the spectator. Ask them to sign their name on the bill in large letters. Take the marker back, cap it, return it to your pocket.

Lean forward slightly, extending your left hand toward the spectator to take the bill. At the same time, **let your right hand drop naturally to your side and catch the cigar from your sleeve.** The tip of the cigar rests against the curled right second fingertip, held lightly by the thumb and third fingertip. Retract the left hand and raise the right hand until they meet directly in front of you, each holding one end of the bill. Though the cigar is concealed in the right hand, the thumb and first fingertip have no trouble grasping the end of the bill. Pull the left end of the bill inward until it touches the right wrist, covering the cigar under the natural action of reading the spectator's signature (tilt upward slightly). Under this cover, **your right third fingertip curls beneath the outer end of the cigar.** The left hand now pulls the left end of the bill forward until it is once again facing the audience, and at the same time, **the right third finger kicks the cigar forward so it remains directly behind the bill.** Both hands work in unison, **rolling the bill around the cigar.** Hold the bill closed with the left hand, freeing the right hand. Reach into your pocket and bring out the lighter.

*(And now for something I've always wanted to do.)* Ignite the lighter and hold it near the right end of the rolled bill. Pause, smile. *(Actually, there are more enjoyable ways to have your money go up in smoke.)* Let the lighter go out and toss it on the table. The right hand returns to the rolled bill and turns palm up just beneath the right end. The left hand tilts the right end downward and **allows the cigar to slide out into view onto the right hand.** *(This is one of them. A Cigar. At one time a symbol of affluence and opulence. Presently a sign of bad habits...which, fortunately for you, I don't possess. I won't smoke it...now.)* Hand the cigar to the spectator (or tuck it into their breast pocket if possible). *(We'll get back to this later.)*

Pick up the lighter and return it to your side jacket pocket, **slipping on the thumbtip** at the same time. The right hand moves to the bill and assists the left hand in unrolling it. Hold the bill facing the audience. Fold the bill in the **Klause Bill Switch** sequence (in half, in quarters, in eighths, in sixteenths). On the third fold, your right thumb extends to the left -- it is in an excellent position to **pick up the thumbtip.** Keep the fingers curled during folding so as much of the bill remains visible as possible. The left thumb presses against the thumbtip to hold it in place as the right thumb is withdrawn. Continue without pausing and make the fourth fold. *(Your twenty-dollar bill. I fold it in half and it gets half the size. Folded in half again, and it gets a quarter the size. Folded in half again and it gets an eighth the size. Folded in half again and it gets pretty boring.)*

**Begin shoving the folded bill to the left, into the thumbtip.** Once the bill and right thumb are partially inside the tip, pull the right hand back and rotate both hands palm-to-palm as if folding the bill in half again -- a mimed action. The tip should now be firmly on the right thumb, folded bill inside it. *(Funny part is, if I fold it once more, it gets so small you can't see it!)* Chuckle. Mime gripping the imaginary tiny-folded bill between the left thumb and fingers, turn that hand palm up. The right hand retracts slightly (thumb held loosely and naturally) and taps the left hand once with the right second finger. Snap the right fingers, open the left hand, and wipe your palms against each other. Assume the classic thumbtip pose (palms toward audience, thumbtips pointing toward audience, second fingers touching thumbtips) for no more than a second, then let the hands drop naturally. *(Sorry about that. I'll make it up to you. To be honest, I already have.)*

Clasp your fingers. *(You remember that a moment ago I rolled the bill into a tube and the cigar came out of the bill. Now the opposite is true.)* Point to the cigar and nod. *(Do me a favor, unwrap the cigar and do something that you may have always wanted to do -- break it in half. Because, inside...)* When the spectator breaks the cigar, it will always break at the proper spot (the point of least resistance where the tobacco was removed). **Take the portion of the cigar containing the bill with your left hand** and rotate it until the protruding bill is in proper position for the Klause Bill Switch (open edges downward). The right thumb and fingertips grasp the right end of the folded bill and **pull it out of the cigar.** The left hand gives the half cigar back to the spectator.

**Execute the second half of the Klause Bill Switch**: As the left hand returns to the right hand, the left second finger moves up beneath the lower edges of the folded bill (keep the left first finger curled so the bill remains in view). **Extend the right thumb to the left, slipping the thumbtip beneath the left thumb.** The left thumb continues pulling the tip to the left while the right thumb moves back to the right. As the right thumb emerges from the tip, it automatically pulls the folded bill out with it -- both folded bills are now one directly behind the other.

The great subtlety of the Klause handling: Insert your left second fingertip into the position indicated to begin unfolding the signed bill. The fingertips and thumbs of both hands **unfold the signed bill while keeping the cigar bill held in place as it swings up and toward you, out of the spectators' sight.** The thumbs slide the innermost bill downward a little until it is level with the thumbtip. The right hand moves inward slightly so you can insert the second finger into the center of the outer bill. Shove the hands toward each other, the right fingertips sliding farther into the folded outer bill and **the right thumb slipping into the thumbtip with the folded bill.** It is vital that the left fingers curl during this so the outer bill remains in view. Extend the left second fingertip to the farthest edge of the outer bill, **pull it open, swinging it to the left so it completely covers the thumbtip.** Use the fingers of both hands to unfold the bill and hold it up until the signature is verified before returning it.

## Key Notes
- The cigar always breaks at the prepared spot because it is the point of least resistance where the tobacco was removed.
- The rolling of the bill around the cigar is a beautiful piece of misdirection -- everything is motivated by the natural action of reading the signature.
- Letting the cigar drop down the sleeve requires the right arm to remain slightly bent. The pickup from the sleeve is masked by the left hand extending for the bill.
- The Klause Bill Switch is what makes this routine extraordinary: the bill appears to remain in full view throughout the unfold. The switch occurs during the unfold itself, when attention is naturally on the emerging signature.
- Practice snapping the fingers with the thumbtip on before performing.
- The classic thumbtip pose should be held for no more than a second -- it is a bit fey if prolonged.

## Credit
Roger Klause (Bill Switch, based on a move by Vlado), Michael Ammar (popularized in Encore 3), Hofzinser (original concept of signed item appearing inside an object)

# Mirror Image
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
Two double-blank cards are used. On one, the spectator writes their initials. The performer writes a word of the spectator's choosing on the same card (supposedly). On the other card, the performer writes a different word and his own initials. When the performer's card is held up to a mirror, the word reads normally in the reflection, but when the card is turned around, the word is revealed to be written completely backward. The spectator's mind registers this as the word having instantly switched places with its mirror image. The same thing then happens with the spectator's own chosen word, an even stronger climax since they chose it themselves.

## Set-up
- A small pocket mirror
- An indelible marker
- A rubber band
- A ball point pen
- A stack of double-blank cards (blank on both sides)
- Prepare: Write the word "thought" on one double-blank card. On a second card, write "thought" backward (letters in reverse order, and the image of each letter reversed as well). Turn over this second card and write your initials in the center of the clean side.
- Stack from the top down: blank card, blank card, backward "thought" card (initials side downward), regular "thought" card (blank side downward), rest of the stack.
- The regular "thought" card must be oriented so the bottoms of the letters are to the right when the packet rests in dealing position. Draw a small arrow on the rubber band with the ball point pen as an orientation guide. Stretch the band around the packet with the arrow pointing away from you (indicating which end should face the audience and that the setup side is on top).
- Store the banded packet, marker, and mirror in a pocket until ready to perform.

## Method and Presentation
Remove the three items from your pocket and drop the marker and mirror on the table (reflective side down). Place the packet into left-hand dealing position (arrow pointing toward the audience), then remove the rubber band and toss it aside.

*(Before we begin, I'd like to get your authorization.)* Thumb off the top double-blank card, turn it over, and hand it to a spectator along with the marker. Ask them to write their initials in the center of one side. While they are busy, **obtain a left pinky break beneath the top card.** When they are done and the ink has dried, take the card and place it, initials side up, on top of the packet. **Tilt the top of the packet toward you and do a Double Turnover**, flipping the top two cards over as one. (The tilt prevents the spectators from seeing the regular "thought" card third from top, and also brings the packet into a more natural writing position.)

*(I want you to name any word in the English language. But I don't have too much room to write here, so make it a three or four letter word -- preferably a not-too-common four letter word.)* Let us assume the spectator says "goat." Repeat the choice aloud, uncap the marker, and **write "goat" on the top card** (supposedly on the other side of the card with the spectator's initials). Deal off the top card and place it on the table so it is clearly visible.

Do a **Triple Turnover** and say *(I'm going to write my initials as well)*, writing your initials on the top card (actually the other side of the regular "thought" card). **Repeat the Triple Turnover, maintaining a left pinky break beneath the three cards** after the turn. Raise the top of the packet toward you and say *(I'm also going to choose a word.)* While you supposedly write "thought," **you actually write the spectator's chosen word, "goat," backward.** This takes practice to do smoothly. While the packet is still tilted toward you, reach into the pinky break with the right first and second fingers and **pull out the card third from the face** (continuing to maintain the break, now beneath two cards). Move this card (which has your initials on the back, as it should) all the way to the right until it clears the packet. Blow on it to help the writing dry, then place it back on the face of the packet. Lower the left hand until the top of the packet is visible again. *(You took a four-letter word, so I wrote a much larger word -- "thought.")* Recap the marker and place it aside.

The right hand picks up the "goat" card and moves beside the packet. *(Traditionally, magicians make two objects change places...)* Tilt both hands toward you for a moment and **openly exchange the "thought" and "goat" cards**, lowering the hands afterward so the words are once again visible. (Tilt back so the surface of the third card is not exposed.) *(...but, instead, I will make these cards change places with themselves, more specifically with the mirror images of themselves.)*

Momentarily rest the "thought" card on top of the packet, jogged to the right where the left thumb holds it in place. The right hand picks up the mirror and turns it reflective side toward the audience for the first time. The right hand returns to the "thought" card, squares it with the packet, and does a **Quadruple Turnover to the break the left pinky has been holding.** Do not worry about the thickness -- you are supposed to be turning over more than one card. Immediately **deal off the top two cards**; your initials are seen on one and the spectator's on the other. The top of the packet is clean, and it is placed aside.

Pick up the mirror with the left hand and hold it broadside toward the audience. Pick up the card with your initials and place it flatly against the mirror, initials side outward. *(Magically the word will change places with its mirror image.)* The right hand moves the card a few inches away from the mirror and tilts it slightly so the spectators can see the reflection. Because "thought" is written backward, **it appears normal in the mirror** -- though this is not immediately apparent to the spectators. **Slowly turn the card around** and let them see "thought" written backward. It takes a second to register, and then the spectators think the word has somehow switched places with the right-side word they saw in the mirror a moment ago. It is disorienting and extremely magical.

**Repeat the handling with the spectator's card and word** to end this unique mystery, and hand both cards out as souvenirs. The mystery and ambiguity of the first transposition will cause the audience to demand to see it again -- and you are in the perfect position to oblige with an even stronger repeat because it happens with the spectator's own chosen word.

## Key Notes
- The magic is entirely psychological -- nothing physically changes. The backward word looks normal in the mirror, and when the spectator sees the backward writing directly, their brain processes it as a transposition having occurred.
- The orientation of the cards is critical: the backward "thought" card must be placed so the bottoms of the letters face the right in dealing position. The rubber band arrow system keeps everything straight.
- Writing backward fluently takes practice. You are writing the spectator's freely chosen word in reverse, in real time, while supposedly writing "thought."
- The Quadruple Turnover is justified by the fact that you are turning over cards with writing on them -- thickness is not suspicious.
- The second phase with the spectator's own word is always stronger than the first phase because the word was freely chosen and written by them.

## Credit
No specific creator credited in the routine description. The optical illusion of backward writing in a mirror is a classic principle.

# Flurious (One-Coin Routine)
Gary Kurtz

## Effect
This is Gary Kurtz's signature stand-up coin routine -- a non-stop manipulative marathon. A half dollar visibly stretches and grows into a jumbo coin, then shrinks back to half-dollar size. The jumbo coin repeatedly vanishes and reappears, travels from hand to hand, penetrates down through the back of the hand, jumps from elbow to elbow Slydini-style in a dizzying cross-arm sequence, and finally transforms into a jumbo Chinese coin beneath a silk. The Chinese coin then vanishes, leaving only the silk, and both hands are shown completely empty.

## Set-up
- A half dollar
- Two three-inch silver jumbo coins
- One three-inch jumbo Chinese coin
- An opaque eighteen-inch silk
- A sport jacket (required)
- Disposition: Tuck the silk into the outer left breast pocket and slip the Chinese coin behind it so it rests on top of the balled-up portion of the silk (keeping the coin near the top of the pocket). Place one jumbo silver coin into the left side jacket pocket. Place the half dollar into the same left side pocket (in the ticket pocket if available, or as far back in the corner as possible to avoid colliding with the jumbo coin). Tuck the other jumbo silver coin into the belt just left of center (concealed by the left side of the jacket). The right side jacket pocket should be empty.

## Method and Presentation

### Part One: The Visible Stretch
Show hands empty. Right hand grasps the left side of the jacket. Turn slightly to the right. **Left Side Jumbo Coin Steal**: The right first and second fingers lightly clip the jacket; the right thumb and second finger (inside the jacket) are level with the belt-tucked jumbo coin. Twist the upper body slightly left so the right hand comes into contact with the coin. Simultaneously, the left hand enters the left side jacket pocket and brings out the half dollar. The right thumb and second finger **grasp the upper portion of the jumbo coin, lift it clear of the belt, and the right second finger curls inward, kicking the jumbo coin upward into Tenkai Palm.** All attention must be directed toward the left hand. Display the half dollar on the palm-up left hand.

*(Actually, this half dollar may be too small for some of you to see, so I'll make it bigger.)* Both hands grasp the coin between thumbs and first fingers. Begin an in-and-out pseudo-bending motion. On the second or third inward bend, the left thumb reaches completely across the back of the half dollar. As both hands bend outward, **the left thumb pulls the half dollar into finger palm while the right fingers straighten, carrying the jumbo coin forward and rotating it broadside toward the audience.** The right thumb moves on top to assist. The coin appears to increase in size instantaneously.

### Part Two: Shrink and Growth
Take the jumbo coin with the left hand to display it. The right hand pushes the left sleeve halfway up the forearm. Transfer the coin to the right hand. Turn the left hand palm down (holding the finger-palmed half dollar) and lower it over the jumbo coin. The instant the jumbo coin is out of sight, **the right second finger curls, kicking the jumbo coin into Tenkai Palm**, while the left fingers contract as if holding the jumbo coin. Turn slightly left. The left hand continues moving away. Extend the right fingers and tap the back of the left hand (a Retention Pass with Jumbo Coin). Slowly squeeze the left fingers into a fist as if crushing the jumbo coin smaller. Crumple the fingers, turn the hand over, and open it to reveal the half dollar. *(I could, of course, make it smaller again.)*

Do a **Shuttle Pass** (Roth): hands are brought beside one another. The left hand turns palm down, retaining the half dollar in finger palm, while the right hand turns palm up, fingers straightening and carrying the jumbo coin beneath the left hand. The right hand immediately turns palm down. The left thumb and fingers grasp the jumbo coin. The right hand lets go and the left hand turns over to display. The jumbo coin is visibly back.

### Part Three: Vanish to Pocket and Reproduction
Repeat the Retention Pass with Jumbo Coin, ostensibly taking the jumbo coin with the left hand. **Insert the left hand into the left front trousers pocket as if putting the jumbo coin away, but actually leave the half dollar behind.** Bring the left hand out empty. At the same time, the right hand pushes up the left sleeve (second time). Face slightly left. The empty left hand pushes up the right sleeve (first time). The right thumb holds the edge of the Tenkai-Palmed coin so all fingers are completely outstretched -- giving the impression both hands are completely empty. Begin to face forward, sliding the left hand down the right arm. As the hands pass over one another, **pivot the jumbo coin into view between them** for a magical production.

### Part Four: Jumbo Ramsay Vanish
Take the jumbo coin with the right hand, holding it at six o'clock (palm up, thumb behind, fingertips in front). The left hand is palm toward you, fingers straight and together, pointed diagonally upward at roughly forty-five degrees. Bring the left hand in front of the jumbo coin, shielding it except for a small portion visible above the left first finger. A split-second later, move the thumb flatly against the upper side of the first finger to completely conceal the coin. At that moment, **the right thumb relaxes and allows the coin to pivot counterclockwise. As the coin spins around, the right third finger moves beneath it and pulls it into Tenkai Palm.** The left hand rotates slightly so it is palm up. Shift the left hand a few inches left and extend the right fingers to tap the backs of the left fingers. Turn the left hand palm toward audience -- the jumbo coin has vanished.

Brush the right fingers down the left hand from palm to fingertips. Maneuver the left palm directly beneath the jumbo coin and **let the coin drop from the right hand into a sort of Gambler's Cop position.** Turn slightly right. The right hand turns palm up, rotating on top of the jumbo coin (based on Vernon's handwashing acquitment). Turn the right hand palm down and move it slightly right. The left second and third fingers **push the jumbo coin upward, pressed against the palm.** Pivot the right palm up toward the audience, spreading the fingers. Reverse the action. The moment the right hand reaches palm-toward-you, the left thumb and fingers straighten, **carrying the jumbo coin flatly behind the right fingers.** Slide the right hand upward to reveal the jumbo coin -- it appears as if the right hand merely turns and moves upward to reveal it.

### Part Five: Triple Vanish and Reproduction Sequence
Pretend to place the jumbo coin into the left hand (Retention Vanish as before). The right thumb moves onto the edge of the palmed jumbo coin, fingers straighten, tap the back of the left hand. Move the right hand a few inches to the right, let the fingers curl naturally so the jumbo coin drops to fingertip rest. **Dart the right hand to the left and reproduce the jumbo coin directly over the palm-down left hand** by straightening the fingers so the coin swings forward broadside. At the moment of reappearance, the left fingers momentarily clench -- the illusion is of plucking the jumbo coin up through the back of the left hand.

Repeat this vanish and reproduction **three times in rapid succession**, each time reproducing the jumbo coin a few inches higher.

### Part Six: Penetration Through the Hand
After the third reproduction, rest the lower edge of the jumbo coin on the back of the left wrist. **Roll the coin up the back of the left hand, simultaneously shoving it into Tenkai Palm.** Without pausing, brush the right fingertips down the back of the left fist, miming the coin penetrating downward. Turn the left hand palm up and spread the fingers -- the hand is empty. Slide the right hand down the length of the left hand. When it passes over the left palm (which bends inward at the wrist), **leave the jumbo coin on the left fingers.** The left hand immediately turns palm down, fingers curling. Turn the right hand palm up and smack the knuckles of the left fist with the backs of the right fingers. Produce the jumbo coin by swinging it downward, broadside, moving the left thumb onto it and straightening the fingers. The overall illusion is of a penetration down through the back of the left hand -- it gets stuck, and you smack it through.

### Part Seven: Slydini Cross-Arm Sequence
Repeat the Jumbo Ramsay Vanish, pretending to take the coin with the left hand. **Cross the arms Slydini-style, left above right**, each hand reaching to the opposite elbow. Turn slightly left. When the left hand reaches the right elbow, it turns palm up and outward to reveal the coin has vanished. A moment later, **the right hand reproduces the jumbo coin from the left elbow** (thumb moves onto the coin, fingers straighten) as you turn slightly right.

Uncross the arms. Repeat the Retention Pass with Jumbo Coin to apparently take the coin with the left hand. **Insert the left hand into the left side jacket pocket** as if getting rid of the coin. The right thumb and first finger momentarily grasp the left side of the jacket to brace it (as during the initial steal). The left hand comes out empty. The right hand darts left and reproduces the jumbo coin from the air.

Repeat the Retention Pass with Jumbo Coin again, inserting the left hand into the left side jacket pocket again. This time, **the left hand grasps the second jumbo coin while it is concealed from view.** As the left hand begins to withdraw, the right hand darts left and reproduces the palmed jumbo coin from the air at shoulder level (Miser's Dream style). The left hand drops naturally to the side, holding the second jumbo coin.

Face forward, right hand displaying a jumbo coin at shoulder level. Raise the left hand to waist level, starting to turn it palm up. The right hand descends to meet it, turning palm down and **swinging its jumbo coin into Tenkai Palm.** When the hands meet, the right first and second fingertips clip the outer edge of the jumbo coin resting in the left hand. Raise the right hand and straighten the fingers, turning the (left-hand) jumbo coin over and lowering it back onto the left palm. Close the left fingers over it. Turn the left hand palm down. Immediately **reproduce the Tenkai-Palmed jumbo coin from the air** directly above the left hand.

Rest the lower edge of the reproduced jumbo coin against the middle of the left forearm and **repeat the roll-up-the-arm vanish.** This time, instantly reproduce the left-hand jumbo coin by moving the left thumb onto it and straightening the thumb and fingers while the hand remains palm down.

The right hand moves to the visible jumbo coin at the left fingertips and clips it between the right first and second fingers. Lay it onto the left palm. Cup the left hand around it and do a **Roth-type Shuttle Pass**, apparently dumping the coin into the right hand (which turns palm up) while the jumbo coin is retained inside the loosely curled fingers of the left hand, which turns palm down.

### Part Eight: Cross-Arm Elbow Sequence
**Cross the arms Slydini-style, right over left**, and tap the right-hand jumbo coin against the left elbow, turning slightly right. On the third tap, **snap the coin into Tenkai Palm** and, a split-second later, reproduce the left-hand coin from the right elbow (bend the left thumb onto it and straighten the fingers), turning slightly left. Straighten the left arm and slide the right hand down it toward the left hand. During this, the left hand turns palm up and flips its jumbo coin flatly onto the palm.

Repeat the Shuttle Pass. **Cross the arms right over left again** and repeat the sequence: the right-hand coin is tapped against the left elbow twice, then snapped into Tenkai Palm. A split-second later, the left hand produces its jumbo coin from the right elbow as you turn back left.

Turn to the right and **reproduce the right hand's jumbo coin from the left elbow.** At the same time, **the left fingers secretly let their jumbo coin drop onto the right side jacket pocket** (Roth's disposal technique). Uncross the arms and transfer the visible jumbo coin to the left hand, turning it over several times as you face forward.

### Part Nine: The Chinese Coin Transformation
The right hand reaches up to the left breast pocket and inserts the second finger and thumb, **grasping the Chinese coin through the silk.** Tilt the hand forward and curl the fingers, pulling the Chinese coin horizontally into the hand (supported from beneath by the curled third finger). The second finger wraps around the edge, pressing through the silk like Curl Palm. **Pull the silk out of the pocket with a forward flourish** so it hangs downward. The concealed jumbo Chinese coin must remain horizontal at all times.

Transfer the visible jumbo coin to the right hand, holding it between the thumb and first finger through the silk. The left hand grasps the center of one silk edge and pulls it out of the right hand. Immediately **cover the right hand with the silk**, so its center lies over the jumbo coins.

**The Changeover Palm**: Bring the hands together until the left fingertips slightly overlap the right fingertips through the silk. The left thumb and first finger grasp the vertical silver coin through the silk, while the left second and third fingers cradle the horizontal Chinese coin from beneath. **Extend the right second finger, carrying the jumbo Chinese coin to the left.** The left hand now holds both jumbo coins through the silk (completely surrounded by it). The right hand turns palm toward the audience and opens -- it is empty.

The right hand grasps the forward hem of the silk and pulls it up over the vertical silver coin, back, exposing that jumbo coin. Thrust both hands forward. In quick succession: retract the left hand until it is directly beneath the right hand, bending it downward at the wrist until the silver coin is horizontal. The right second and third fingertips **nip the forward edge of the silver coin, taking it into the right hand.** Without pausing, the right hand descends about a foot until the shape of the Chinese coin can be seen pressed against the silk. These actions must occur as a single flowing movement.

**The climax**: Rapidly raise the right hand, pulling the forward portion of the silk up and over the Chinese coin (the silver coin in the right hand remains horizontal). The left hand moves forward and upward, tilting the Chinese coin to vertical. As the right hand continues inward, **the silver coin is guided into the left breast pocket** (covered from the front by the silk and Chinese coin). The right hand, now empty of the silver coin, lets go of the silk and lifts the Chinese coin out of the left hand, flashing both sides.

### Part Ten: The Final Vanish
The left hand readjusts its grip on the silk (thumb and first finger firmly grasp a portion, curled pinky grips another, keeping the silk spread). Turn the left hand palm down and move it forward so the hanging silk shifts in front of the Chinese coin. **Allow the Chinese coin to slide down the left sleeve.** Raise the right hand as if pushing the Chinese coin up to the center of the silk. The left hand lets go and moves out from beneath the silk. The left hand, fingers together and straight, grasps the portion of the silk resting over the right hand flatly inside it, as if it still contains the Chinese coin. Pull the silk off the right hand and raise the left hand above shoulder level. The right hand grasps a dangling corner and **jerks the silk out of the left hand.** The silk flutters down. Both hands are shown empty.

### Cleanup
Fold the silk in half in the left hand. Drop the left hand to your side as you bow for applause, and **allow the previously-sleeved jumbo silver coin to slide out of the sleeve into the left hand.** Casually fold the silk over the jumbo coin and slip the whole thing into the left breast pocket, behind the Chinese coin. If properly wrapped, the coins will not rub together or make noise in the pocket.

## Key Notes
- This is an extremely difficult routine requiring a great deal of practice to master. It bears little resemblance to close-up coin magic and is far closer in technique and performance style to stand-up/platform routines such as The Miser's Dream.
- The Tenkai Palm with jumbo coins requires active finger support -- the second or third fingers are often loosely curled beneath the coin to keep it horizontal, freeing the thumb for greater mobility.
- The Jumbo Ramsay Vanish (Part Four) relies on the coin pivoting counterclockwise behind the left hand while the right third finger catches and Tenkai Palms it. The continuous contact of the right first finger with the left pinky sells the illusion.
- The Retention Pass with Jumbo Coin (used throughout) is: left hand descends over right-hand coin, right second finger kicks it into Tenkai Palm as the left hand mimes taking it away. The right hand then taps the back of the left hand with straightened fingers.
- In the cross-arm Slydini sequences, the timing of snapping a coin into Tenkai Palm while simultaneously producing another coin from the opposite elbow requires precise split-second coordination.
- "Soft" coins (worn down to slide noiselessly) are essential throughout. The various palms (Tenkai, Curl, finger palm, classic palm) must be mastered in both hands with jumbo coins.
- Audience management: always direct attention to the hand that is doing something visible, allowing the other hand to work secretly.
- The final sleeve vanish of the Chinese coin works because the silk acts as a screen and the left hand's grip keeps it spread wide enough to hide the action.
