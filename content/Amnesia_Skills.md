## Introduction

**Amnesia** (Greek for "partial or total loss of memory") collects effects using the Mnemonica memorized deck by Juan Tamariz, **without requiring the performer to memorize the stack**. The book is the result of over fifteen years of discussions within the East Blue brainstorming group (Arnaud Chevrier, Frantz Réjasse, Tony Corgann, Isidore Buc).

The book has two sections:
- **Routine**: Five sequential effects where each effect's ending deck order becomes the next effect's starting order, progressing five steps ahead of audience perception.
- **Bonus**: Four standalone effects based on the classic Mnemonica order.

## Preparation: Mnemonica From New Deck Order

### Effect
N/A — preparatory procedure.

### Set-up
A brand new deck (Ace to King of Hearts, Ace to King of Clubs, King to Ace of Diamonds, King to Ace of Spades), jokers and ad cards removed.

### Method and Presentation

**Face-up overhand shuffle** — peel all Spades one by one, reversing their order. **Peel a block of about a dozen cards** aiming to split around the 10 of Diamonds; adjust to take exactly all Diamonds. **Peel all Clubs one by one**, reversing their order. **Left fingers flip the left hand's 26-card block toward the left**, allowing the right hand to drop its block behind — the deck is now in stay-stack.

**Cut to locate the King of Hearts** on the face of the top half, perform an **out faro**. Repeat cutting to **Ace of Diamonds**, out faro. Repeat cutting to **7 of Hearts**, out faro. Repeat cutting to **4 of Diamonds**, out faro.

**Spread to locate the 9 of Clubs and 9 of Spades** in the middle, square with a thumb break between them. **Face-down overhand shuffle**: peel all cards above the break one by one (26 cards), then left fingers flip the block and right hand drops its block behind.

**Cut to see the 2 of Clubs** on the face of the top portion, maintain a break. Cut top third at the King of Diamonds, **out faro** the top third into the middle third. **Transfer the top nine cards as a block under the deck.** The 9 of Diamonds is now the bottom card; the deck is in Mnemonica order. (Under one minute with practice.)

### Key Notes
The entire sequence from new deck to Mnemonica takes about 45 seconds.

### Credit
Juan Tamariz, *Mnemonica* (2004), with Vincent Hedan's handling variations.

## Preparation: Modified Mnemonica

### Set-up
The deck must be in classic Mnemonica order. The **Queen of Clubs** receives a corner crimp (one index bent toward the back). The **9 of Diamonds** receives a **breather crimp**: apply pinched/folded pressure along both diagonals (upper left↔lower right, then upper right↔lower left) to create a gentle pyramid-like bend — invisible at edges but easy to cut to by feel. (Pit Hartling's variation of the breather crimp, originally from Larry Jennings via Ted Lesley, described in Dai Vernon's *The Vernon Chronicles Volume 1*.)

### Method and Presentation
From classic Mnemonica face-up:
1. **Spread first six face cards**, pull the 6 of Clubs, Ace of Hearts, 9 of Diamonds, and 7 of Clubs toward the right, then replace on face — the 7 of Clubs is now between 10 of Diamonds and 6 of Clubs.
2. **Take first five face cards**, spread ~35 cards to the Queen of Clubs, pull the five cards plus Queen of Clubs right, replace — Queen of Clubs is between Queen of Spades and 10 of Diamonds.
3. **Spread to 9 of Clubs**, spread ~37 cards to Ace of Spades, pull from Jack of Spades to 9 of Diamonds plus Ace of Spades right, replace — Ace of Spades between 9 of Clubs and Jack of Spades.
4. **Spread to Jack of Clubs**, push entire deck minus the back four cards against right fingertips. Left hand puts 3 of Clubs on top, then the other three cards (4 of Clubs, 2 of Hearts, 7 of Diamonds). Left hand takes the deck in fork of thumb; right hand takes block from 7 of Spades to 9 of Diamonds and places on top.
5. **Spread to King of Spades**, cull and insert it between 3 of Hearts and 8 of Diamonds.
6. **Spread to 8 of Spades**, maintain 2 of Diamonds/Jack of Hearts/3 of Spades. Cull King of Clubs, insert between 3 of Spades and 8 of Spades.
7. **Take first eight face cards** (to the three Queens), spread to 8 of Hearts. Left hand takes first seven back cards and places on top.
8. **Transfer top six cards under the deck.**
9. **Klondyke shuffle**: peel top card (Ace of Hearts), pull top+bottom pairs six times (13 cards total), place under deck.
10. **Cut at the 5 of Clubs** (13th from back) to face.
11. **Cut at 3 of Diamonds** (center), maintain break above face-up 3 of Diamonds. **Face-down overhand shuffle**: peel 26 cards above the break one by one, right hand drops its half on top. Jack of Diamonds is now on face.
12. **Cut at Ace of Hearts** (13th from back), **partial out faro** with this 13-card block. **Complete in faro** cutting on the 5 of Clubs.

The deck is now in modified Mnemonica order. (~60 seconds.)

### Key Notes
Total preparation from a new deck: ~2 minutes. Alternatively, keep the order written down and arrange the deck directly by consulting a list.

## 1. First Chance

### Effect
A spectator chooses a card, which is lost in the deck. After shuffling and cutting, cards are dealt into two piles. The magician repeatedly deals his own pile between the spectator and himself, each time halving his chances of holding the chosen card — from 50% to 25% to 12.5% to 6.25% to 3.125%. The magician ends with a single face-down card, which the spectator turns over: it is the chosen card.

### Set-up
Deck in **modified Mnemonica** order. The Queen of Clubs (22nd from top) has a corner crimp.

### Method and Presentation
*(Script not provided in original — the effect proceeds with probability narration.)*

**Force the Queen of Clubs** using the corner crimp to obtain a break, then riffle force (or Dani Daortiz' variation of the classic force). Spectator looks at and replaces the card at the same spot. Deck order is unchanged.

**False cut and/or false shuffle.** Left hand holds deck face down in dealing position. **Deal two piles regularly**, starting with the spectator.

*"Each of us now has a 50% chance of having the chosen card in their pile. Since I'm a nice person, I will increase your chances by dealing my pile again."*

Take your pile and **deal it again**, starting with spectator, piling on top of their first pile. You now have a 13-card pile.

*"Now I only have a 25% chance... This is still too much in my favor, so let's deal again."*

Take your pile, deal again starting with spectator. You now have 6 cards (6 of Clubs, 7 of Clubs, 10 of Diamonds, Queen of Clubs, Queen of Spades, Queen of Diamonds from back).

*"Now I only have a 12.5% chance. And if I deal again, I will be at 6.25%."*

Take your pile, deal again. You now have 3 cards (Queen of Diamonds, Queen of Clubs, 7 of Clubs from back).

*"Once again, and I'll be at 3.125%. Which is just one card."*

Take your pile, deal again. You are left with a single card — the Queen of Clubs.

*"Only one chance out of fifty-two that this randomly isolated card is actually your card."*

Let the spectator turn it face up.

### Key Notes
Based on L. Vonsburgh Lyons' "Last Chance" (*The Jinx* No. 54, 1939). The dealing procedure exploits the predictability of card displacement — the chosen card is controlled to the 22nd position. Optionally reverse the effect by having the spectator deal (starting with the magician, the spectator ends up with their own card). After the revelation, place the Queen of Clubs under the deck. The deck order is now set for the next effect.

### Credit
L. Vonsburgh Lyons, "Last Chance", *The Jinx* No. 54, 1939. Vincent Hedan's adaptation to Mnemonica. Force variation by Dani Daortiz, *Utopia* (2011).

## 2. Feminine Perspective

### Effect
*"Since you've chosen a Queen, let's try something together with the Queens in the deck."* Spectator cuts the deck in half and takes one packet, magician takes the other. Both turn their top cards face up — 7 of Clubs and 3 of Diamonds. The magician claims a "perfect match," then suggests exchanging packets might help with "perspective." After exchanging, the spectator's card transforms into the Queen of Hearts and the magician's into the Queen of Diamonds. This is repeated: 3 of Diamonds and 10 of Diamonds transform into the Queen of Spades and Queen of Clubs. All four Queens are produced.

### Set-up
Deck in the order resulting from "First Chance." A mat is recommended.

### Method and Presentation
*"Since you've chosen a Queen, let's try something together with the Queens in the deck."*

Place deck face down, ask spectator to cut about half. Take the bottom part, **transfer the three bottom cards to the top** (pass or double undercut).

*"Let's both turn our top card face up, I think you might have created an interesting coincidence."*

Spectator turns his top card: 7 of Clubs. You perform a **double turnover**: 3 of Diamonds.

*"Ah, you see, our cards are a perfect match, it's unbelievable! No, it doesn't seem obvious to you? Maybe it has to do with perspective. Let's turn our cards face down and exchange packets, you will see what I mean."*

Spectator turns his card face down; you do a **double turnover**. Exchange packets.

*"Let's both turn our top card face up… Can you see better now?"*

Spectator's card: Queen of Hearts. You perform a **double turnover**: Queen of Diamonds. Ask spectator to confirm the two red Queens.

*"Put your red Queen on the table and let's try again."*

Right hand takes the double card in Biddle grip, **drops it face up on the mat** (gently bending its face so it remains squared). Spectator places his Queen next to his pile.

*"Let's both turn our top card face up."*

Repeat: spectator turns 3 of Diamonds, you do **double turnover** showing 10 of Diamonds.

*"Ah, you see, another perfect coincidence! No, you still can't see it? Let's exchange our packets, it might help you."*

Spectator turns card face down, you do double turnover. Exchange. Spectator turns Queen of Spades, you do **double turnover**: Queen of Clubs.

*"Unbelievable, you and I managed to find the four Queens in a magical way!"*

**Clean-up (restoring deck order):** Take spectator's packet, place on top of yours. **Spread bottom quarter, spot bottom five cards, get left pinky break above them.** Right hand **cuts top four cards** (cutting under the 9 of Diamonds breather crimp), places face down on table. Right hand takes Queen of Spades' small packet by front edge, places three cards face down on tabled packet. Right hand **cuts everything above the break**, places on tabled pile. Right hand takes Queen of Clubs' packet, rotates face down, places on tabled pile. Right hand takes five-card packet from left hand, places face down on top. The Queens are "lost" and the deck order is restored for the next effect.

### Key Notes
A mat facilitates picking up packets and managing double cards. Alternatives: use card case, cigarette pack, smartphones, or glasses as supports. Performing on the floor helps hide double card thickness via the audience's downward line of vision. To skip "Feminine Perspective" and go directly to "Four Amnesiac Errors": cut top seven cards (under breather crimp), table them; spread bottom quarter, get break above bottom eight cards; cut above break onto tabled pile; place remaining cards on top.

### Credit
Adapted from Peter Duffie, "Illusion VS Reality", *Card School* (1995). Vincent Hedan's adaptation to Mnemonica.

## 3. Four Amnesiac Errors

### Effect
*"We found the four Queens together, now let's see if you are able to find the four Aces on your own."* The spectator cuts the deck into four piles following simple step-by-step instructions. The top card of each pile is turned over, revealing the 3 of Clubs, 4 of Clubs, 2 of Hearts, and 7 of Diamonds — apparently four failures. But then the magician counts down into the deck: at the 3rd card is the Ace of Diamonds, at the 4th card is the Ace of Clubs, at the 2nd card is the Ace of Spades, at the 7th card is the Ace of Hearts. The spectator's four "errors" actually revealed the positions of the Aces.

### Set-up
Deck in the order resulting from "Feminine Perspective."

### Method and Presentation
*"We have found the four Queens together, now let's see if you are able to find the four Aces on your own."*

While talking, **go through deck, find the Jack of Clubs** (22nd from face), **cut to have Jack of Clubs on the face**. Place deck face down.

*"When you want to do something difficult, a good trick consists in dividing this task in several, smaller, easier-to-follow steps. So do not focus too much on the end goal, and simply cut the deck roughly into two halves."*

**Point at top of deck, then at a spot about two card-widths to the right** — guiding the spectator's cut. The lower half (left) and top half (right) are separated by about a card width.

*"Perfect, now cut each pile into two."*

**Point at top of lower half, then at the empty space between piles.** Spectator cuts left packet into two, placing the quarter between the two existing piles.

*"Finally, cut this pile into two."*

**Point at top of right packet, then at the empty space to its right.** Spectator cuts right packet into two. Label packets A, B, C, D from left to right.

*"You see, it is easier step by step, and you should have successfully cut on the four Aces. Let's check. Would you say you're usually lucky?"*

**Take packet D** in left hand in dealing position. **Take top card of D, offset slightly forward-right. Take top card of C, place on D's top card, spread right. Take top card of B, place similarly. Take top card of A, place similarly.** Left hand holds packet D with a small four-card fan on top. Right hand takes this fan (thumb on top, fingers under). **Left thumb peels top card of fan onto left packet, pinky gets break. Peel next two cards one by one.** Right hand holds one card, turns it face up: **3 of Clubs** — appears to be a failure.

*"A small error, nothing too serious. Maybe you succeeded in finding the other three Aces."*

**Right hand takes four cards above break in Biddle grip**, places this squared block on packet A, and **immediately spreads the whole pile toward you** (ditching hidden cards under the 3 of Clubs).

**Turn over top card of packet D: 4 of Clubs.**

*"OK, I'll admit, it is difficult. Maybe you succeeded in finding the two other Aces."*

Right hand takes 4 of Clubs, places on packet B, **spreads pile toward you**.

**Turn over top card of packet D: 2 of Hearts.**

*"Too bad, but if you manage to find even only one Ace, that will already be impressive."*

Right hand places 2 of Hearts on packet C, **spreads pile toward you**.

**Turn over top card of packet D: 7 of Diamonds.** Place entire packet D to the right of the others, spread slightly toward yourself.

*"Would you say you're usually lucky?"*

Discuss spectator's luck while casually preparing the finale. **Both hands take face-up cards**, place them further forward forming a face-up row. **Left hand takes packet A**, pinky gets break under top card. Right hand takes packet D. Under pretense of squaring, right hand places its packet on left hand's (break maintained). Right hand goes to packet C, then back to left hand, **takes all cards above break and places on packet C**. Left pinky gets new break under top card. Right lifts combined C pile onto left hand's packet (break maintained). Right hand squares packet B. Right hand **takes cards above break, puts on packet B**, lifts combined B pile onto left hand's packet. Deck is reassembled.

*"I think that you did something very special. Look, you cut on a 3, and if we look at the third card down the deck… it is an Ace!"*

**Deal top three cards face up** under the 3 of Clubs — third card is the Ace of Diamonds.

Move to 4 of Clubs: **deal top four cards** — fourth card is Ace of Clubs. Repeat for 2 of Hearts (Ace of Spades at 2nd card) and 7 of Diamonds (Ace of Hearts at 7th card). Progressively accelerate dealing to intensify impact.

*"In the end, you successfully found the four Aces, in your own way!"*

**Clean-up:** Right hand picks up Ace of Spades, brandishes it, then places it face up to the right. Gather: Ace of Hearts pile on 9 of Clubs, lift and put on Ace of Clubs, place on Ace of Diamonds, all face down on deck. **Cut under 9 of Diamonds (breather crimp)**. Take 3 of Clubs on 7 of Diamonds, on 2 of Hearts, on 4 of Clubs. Left pinky gets break under top two cards. Place four-card group on deck (break maintained). **Insert Ace of Spades face down into break** (similar to a Tilt, but done openly).

### Key Notes
Inspired by Arnaud Chevrier's unpublished routine. Method adapted from Edward Marlo's "Method 37" (*M.I.N.T.*, 1964-1965) and Marc Delahousse (*Secrets à la carte*, 1993), which in turn was inspired by Jim Surprise/Jerry Mentzer (*Card Cavalcade*, 1972). The spectator-cutting guidance prevents problems: laypeople can't estimate quarters, order/position of packets must be controlled, and guiding them step-by-step appears helpful rather than controlling. After this effect, the deck is closest to original Mnemonica — only the 8 of Diamonds differs in the first half, enabling many half-stack tricks.

### Credit
Arnaud Chevrier (unpublished inspiration), Edward Marlo "Method 37" (*M.I.N.T.*, 1964-1965), Marc Delahousse (*Secrets à la carte*, 1993), Jerry Mentzer (*Card Cavalcade*, 1972). Vincent Hedan's adaptation.

## 4. Kingdom Hearts

### Effect
Despite the deck having been used for multiple effects before, the magician produces all thirteen Hearts from the deck in numerical order (Ace through King), finishing with all four Kings as a bonus.

### Set-up
Deck in the order resulting from "Four Amnesiac Errors."

### Method and Presentation
*"Being able to find the four Aces in the deck like you did, this is quite impressive and it resembles the training followed by some gamblers and cheaters. They practice being able to locate the four Aces, or all the cards in a suit. Even more difficult, they can try to find all the cards in the same suit, in numerical order! For example, with the Hearts from Ace to King, it would look like this..."*

**Ace of Hearts:** **Bottom slip cut** — left hand buckles bottom card, right hand takes all cards except bottom in Biddle grip, **swing cut**. Turn right hand palm up to reveal the Ace of Hearts on face. Left hand takes Ace, places it face up on table. Right hand puts its packet between buckled bottom card and rest of left packet.

**2 of Hearts:** Turn top card face up — 4 of Clubs (apparent mistake). Left pinky gets break under second card (2 of Hearts). Perform **Jerry K. Hartman's Blow Away Change** (or Shapeshifter Change, or double turnover + Kardyro-Marlo move) to transform 4 of Clubs into 2 of Hearts. Place face-up 2 of Hearts on tabled Ace, slightly spread right.

**3 of Hearts:** **Estimated cut of ~27 cards** to cut under/above 3 of Hearts. Complete cut, show 3 of Hearts, place face up on pile. **Cut under 9 of Diamonds (breather crimp)**. (Small margin of error of 1-2 cards, adjustable at last second or correctable with double/triple turnover.)

**4 of Hearts:** *"We are looking for the 4 of Hearts, so let's count to four."* With 9 of Diamonds on face, **count top four cards face up** — fourth is the 4 of Hearts. Place on table. Left pinky gets break under top two cards. Right hand takes three indifferent cards, places back on top of deck.

**5 of Hearts:** **Double undercut** to transfer five cards above break to face. Turn top card: 5 of Hearts. Place on table.

**6 of Hearts:** Recap: *"We found the Ace, whose value is 1."* Deal top card face up. *"Then we found the 2, whose value is 2."* **Spread top two cards (without changing order), turn over as block onto first card.** Continue: *"We found the 3... the 4... the 5..."* When placing the five-card group face up, the face card is the 6 of Hearts. Place on table. Gather face-up pile, place under deck (8 of Spades now on face).

**7 of Hearts:** Turn top card: 10 of Clubs (apparent mistake). *"The 10 of Clubs… and if you add this 10 to the 6 we have just found, we get 16!"* **Spread deck face down, count to the 16th card** — the 7 of Hearts. Place on table. 10 of Clubs back on top.

**8 of Hearts:** *"Now we are looking for the 8."* Square deck, put **face up on table** — shows 8 of Spades. *"Not this 8; we are looking for the 8 of Hearts."* Left hand holds deck face up, **pivot wrist to face down**. **Drop eight cards from face one by one**, counting aloud. Pivot deck face up — the 8 of Hearts is revealed on the face. Place on table. Eight dropped cards back on top.

**9 of Hearts:** **Double undercut** to transfer top two cards to bottom. Turn top card: 9 of Hearts.

**10 of Hearts:** **Estimated cut of ~18 cards** to cut above/under 10 of Hearts. Show and place. Alternative: cut under breather crimp, show 9 of Diamonds, **spell "N-I-N-E-D-I-A-M-O-N-D-S"** dealing face cards one by one, creating a face-down pile. At end, face card of deck is the 10 of Hearts. Place used cards face up on face of deck.

**Jack of Hearts:** **Cut above Queen of Clubs (corner crimp)**. Spell **"J-A-C-K"** dealing top cards face up — turn next card: Jack of Hearts. Place used cards face down on deck.

**Queen of Hearts:** **Bottom slip cut** (as for Ace of Hearts) to produce the Queen of Hearts. Right hand puts packet back between buckled card and rest.

**King of Hearts:** **Face-down overhand shuffle**: transfer top three cards under deck by running one by one. Spell **"T-H-E"** dealing three cards face down, **"K-I-N-G"** dealing four cards face down to its right, **"O-F"** dealing two cards, **"H-E-A-R-T-S"** dealing six cards. Turn over last dealt card: King of Hearts. *"But kings like to stay among themselves..."* Turn top card of each previous pile: all Kings!

### Key Notes
This is the first effect Hedan developed for Mnemonica and the one that motivated him to find other ideas to perform before it, since this finale destroys the deck arrangement. The deck can still be exploited afterward. **Clean-up for next effect:** Place four Kings on deck in any order. Place "HEARTS" pile on "OF" pile, both on "KING" pile, all on "THE" pile, everything on deck. Take Hearts Ace through Queen and place under deck. The deck is now ready for "Bored Amnesia."

### Credit
Vincent Hedan. Jerry K. Hartman, "Blow Away Change" (*The Minotaur* Vol. 6 No. 1, 1994). Marc DeSouza, "Shapeshifter Change" (*The Magic Of Doug Conn*, 1999). Tony Kardyro & Edward Marlo, "The K.M. Move" (1962).

## 5. Bored Amnesia

### Effect
An adaptation of Simon Aronson's "Shuffle-bored." The spectator mixes cards face up and face down several times, yet the magician predicts: the number of face-down cards (29), the number of black cards among them (18), that all red cards are Diamonds (11), and one anomaly — the King of Hearts appears among the Diamonds, which was also predicted.

### Set-up
Deck in the order resulting from "Kingdom Hearts." The 9 of Diamonds (breather crimp) is 29th from top.

### Method and Presentation
*(Not described in full detail — the author recommends reading Simon Aronson's and Aldo Colombini's originals.)*

**Cut under the 9 of Diamonds** to create the two packets used for the "Shuffle-bored" procedure. The four predictions are: 29 face-down cards, 18 black cards, 11 Diamonds, plus the King of Hearts anomaly.

### Key Notes
Vincent Hedan discovered this effect under Aldo Colombini's title "Pre-deck-ability" and performs Colombini's presentation with Aronson's methodology.

### Credit
Simon Aronson, "Shuffle-Bored", *Bound To Please* (1994). Aldo Colombini (presentation). Vincent Hedan's adaptation to Mnemonica.

## 6. Amnesiac Tut Tut

### Effect
A deck is shuffled. The magician goes through the face-up deck, turning the two black Kings face down where found. A spectator chooses a card, which is returned and the deck shuffled again. Spread face down, the two face-up black Kings have gathered in the center, sandwiching one card — the chosen card.

### Set-up
Deck in classic Mnemonica order. 9 of Diamonds is a breather crimp.

### Method and Presentation

**First Version (force and searching pair):**
**Perform an out faro** (cutting under King of Diamonds). 9 of Diamonds stays on face. **Go through face-up deck**, turn King of Clubs face down, continue to King of Spades, turn it face down. Square.

Left hand holds deck face down. **Right thumb riffles bottom cards** to obtain break above bottom five. Right hand lifts deck in Biddle grip, **right first finger does swing cut of top half** into left hand, which places it under deck. Right thumb maintains break near middle, above 6 of Clubs. **Force 6 of Clubs** (riffle or classic force). Spectator replaces card in opening, close deck.

**Cut under 9 of Diamonds (breather crimp). Perform out faro** (cutting under Ace of Diamonds). **Another out faro** (cutting under Jack of Hearts). **Cut top quarter to bottom.** Spread deck face down — the two black Kings have gathered, sandwiching one card: the 6 of Clubs.

**Second Version (free choice and searching pair):**
**Perform out faro.** **Cut bottom four cards to top** — 6 of Clubs (force card) now on face. **Go through face-up deck**, turn both black Kings face down. **Spread face down, spectator freely chooses a card** (not a face-up King). While spectator looks at card, **left fingers push the bottom card of the left spread (6 of Clubs) toward the right; right fingers add it to the face of the right spread** — moving the 6 of Clubs to the selection position. Right hand places its cards on left, square.

Spread again for spectator to return card. **Control chosen card to bottom** (cull under spread or Tamariz Perpendicular Control). **Cut 9 of Diamonds under deck.** Do two out faros, cut top quarter to bottom, spread to reveal sandwich.

**Third Version (four of a kind — two chosen cards found by four Queens):**
**Do three out faros. Go through face-up deck, turn all four Queens face down.** Hold face down. Spread, visually spot Queen of Spades, then the third face-down card above it (7 of Diamonds). Square with break above it, **force 7 of Diamonds** on first spectator. Spread again, spot Queen of Hearts, then third face-down card below it (4 of Hearts), square with break, **force 4 of Hearts** on second spectator. **Do two out faros.** Spread face down — red Queens sandwich the 7 of Diamonds, black Queens sandwich the 4 of Hearts.

### Key Notes
Based on Michael Close's "Tut Tut" (*Workers 5*, 1996), which used the Aronson stack and faros. Arnaud Chevrier adapted it to Mnemonica replacing faros with antifaros (2005). Hedan contributed additional sandwich discoveries. To restore Mnemonica order: for each out faro performed, you need one out antifaro; for three out faros, perform three out antifaros or five out faros. Additional sandwich pairs available at different faro levels: red 7's capture 8 of Spades, black 6's capture Ace of Spades, red 3's capture Jack of Hearts. Analysis of all 52 in-faro levels is provided, including two cases where four Queens capture two cards (18 or 44 in faros).

## 7. Amnesiac Blackjack

### Effect
The spectator freely chooses the number of players (3-8) and which player should win. Despite these free choices and the deck being shuffled, the designated player receives a perfect blackjack hand — an Ace and a 10 totaling 21.

### Set-up
Deck in classic Mnemonica order. Queen of Clubs has a corner crimp; 9 of Diamonds is a breather crimp.

### Method and Presentation
*"Do you know how to play blackjack? For those who don't, here is a quick explanation..."*

While talking, left hand holds Mnemonica stack face up:
1. **Spread a dozen face cards**, find **Ace of Clubs** (10th from face), take it out, show it, place back on face.
2. **Cut on Queen of Clubs (corner crimp)** so it is on the back.
3. **Spread middle of deck face up, spot 8 of Clubs. Cull Queen of Clubs under spread**, insert between 8 of Clubs and 10 of Spades. **Cull 8 of Clubs** under spread, control to back. (Queen of Clubs and 8 of Clubs have swapped positions.)
4. **Spread face up, spot Ace of Diamonds** around middle, **cut so Ace of Diamonds is on face.**

*"How many players do you want: three, four, five, six, seven or eight?"*

**Simple Transfer:** Depending on spectator's answer, **transfer that many cards from bottom to top** (riffle count break, then cut at break or pass). Never more than 8 cards.

*"And among these [N] players, who should be the winner?"*

**Transfer winner's position number of cards from bottom to top.** Optional false shuffle before dealing.

**Deal first card to each player face up** in a row. **Deal second card to each player face up** on top of the first, offset rearward. The winner's second card is dealt **face down** to preserve mystery. Turn it over: the Ace of Diamonds with a 10-value card — perfect blackjack!

**Clean-up:** Take both cards from rightmost hand (without squaring), place on neighboring hand, cascade everything together without squaring. Left hand pinches front part against top of deck; right hand takes rear part, separating interlaced cards, puts on top. Turn block face down. This cancels the dealing procedure.

**Minimalist Transfer (preferred method):**
After swapping 8 of Clubs and Queen of Clubs, **locate 8 of Diamonds** (three cards beyond the Queen of Clubs's new position), **cut so 8 of Diamonds is on face.** Ask for number of players (P) and winner's position (W). **Calculate P + W - 10 = cards to transfer.** Positive result = add cards from bottom to top. Zero = no transfer. Negative = remove cards from top to bottom (transfer to bottom). This moves 0-6 cards instead of 4-16, with 12% of cases requiring no adjustment at all.

**To restore Mnemonica:** Cut Queen of Clubs to back, swap 8 of Clubs and Queen of Clubs positions. Cut 9 of Diamonds to face. Reposition Ace of Clubs between 4 of Diamonds and 9 of Clubs (10 cards from face).

### Key Notes
The method works for any number of players (3-8) and any winner position. The simple two-part adjustment is designed to avoid calculations; the Minimalist Transfer is more elegant. About 10 different adjustment approaches exist.

Vincent Hedan (Mnemonica analysis and method).

## 8. Murphy's Amnesiac Spelling

### Effect
A spectator chooses a card (10 of Diamonds), which is lost in a shuffled deck. She spells its name dealing one card per letter. The last card is a 10 — but with the wrong suit. She tries twice more — each time, a 10 of a different suit. With very few cards left, the fourth and final spelling reveals the 10 of Diamonds, and she had exactly enough cards for this final round.

### Set-up
Deck in classic Mnemonica order.

### Method and Presentation

**Preparation:** Left hand holds Mnemonica stack face up. **Spread first four face cards**, right fingers can touch back of 10 of Diamonds. Left thumb maintains 6 of Clubs and Ace of Hearts; right hand pulls 9 of Diamonds and 10 of Diamonds right, then replaces — 10 of Diamonds is now between Ace of Hearts and 9 of Diamonds. **Spread a dozen cards, visually locate 10 of Spades. Cull it under spread.** Spread ~20 more cards to Queen of Hearts and 3 of Diamonds. **Insert 10 of Spades between them.** Square, hold face down, left pinky secures break above 10 of Diamonds (2nd from face).

**Force 10 of Diamonds** (any force not disturbing order; Hedan uses Dani Daortiz' variation of classic force with break). Spectator looks at card, replaces at same spot. Square while maintaining break under 10 of Diamonds. **Double undercut to control 10 of Diamonds under deck.** Optional false shuffle.

Hand deck to spectator. *"The identity of your card will allow you to find it on your own."*

Spectator announces "Ten of Diamonds." **Invite her to spell "T-E-N-O-F-D-I-A-M-O-N-D-S"** dealing one card per letter face up. Last card is a 10 — wrong suit. Repeat twice more — each time a different 10. Few cards remain.

*"Once again..."* She spells one last time. Not only did she have exactly the right number of cards, but the last card is the 10 of Diamonds.

**Clean-up:** Take 10 of Spades packet on 10 of Diamonds packet, put on 10 of Hearts, put on 10 of Clubs. Spread middle of deck to locate 8 of Clubs, square with break above 8 of Clubs. **Slip cut 10 of Spades into break** (original Mnemonica position). Cut above 9 of Diamonds (breather crimp). Spread first four face cards to spot 10 of Diamonds, **left thumb pulls it between Queen of Spades and 6 of Clubs.** Deck is back in Mnemonica.

**Alternative with 7 of Hearts:** Cull 7 of Clubs and 7 of Spades under spread, insert them at specific positions, force 7 of Hearts. Same quadruple spelling effect. Different clean-up procedure provided.

### Key Notes
Hedan originally developed this effect with his own stack (every 13 cards); this is the Mnemonica adaptation. Alternative control: use Tamariz Perpendicular Control to control the forced card to bottom after genuinely returning it anywhere in the deck.

Vincent Hedan, "Murphy's Spelling", *Your Mind Will Always Be My Playground* (2020). Mnemonica adaptation by Hedan.

## 9. Amnesiac Joffe Poker

### Effect
The magician shuffles the deck, then never touches it again. The spectator freely cuts the deck wherever they want (multiple times if desired), chooses the number of players (2-8), and participants join the table. The spectator (or a designated dealer) deals a complete Texas Hold'em game following real rules (burn, flop, turn, river). Despite these impossible conditions, the magician always receives the best hand and wins.

### Set-up
**Permanent preparation (done once):** The entire Mnemonica deck must be **marked on the backs** using thin-tipped Sharpie pens matching the card color. The Bicycle rider back is divided into seven zones (2-8), each containing a number of visual elements:

- Zone 2: 2 birds
- Zone 3: 3 white notches
- Zone 4: 4 birds
- Zone 5: 5 white notches
- Zone 6: 6 white notches
- Zone 7: 7 birds
- Zone 8: 8 petals

For each card in the stack, a specific element in each zone is colored to disappear, indicating the dealer position for that number of players. A complete table of markings for all 52 cards is provided in the book. Some cards have multiple elements marked in a zone (e.g., 9 of Diamonds in zone 7 is marked "1 and 5"), meaning either of those positions can be dealer — an additional free choice.

### Method and Presentation
*"Do you know how to play Texas Hold'em? Perfect, you will be the referee of our poker game. This way, the audience knows that I am following the strict rules of the World Championship and that I am not making any of this up. In a moment you will decide on the number of players, between two and eight players. You will also cut the deck anywhere you want and we will start dealing exactly from the spot you chose in the deck. Finally, I promise you that I will never touch the cards. Despite these impossible conditions, I will still receive the best hand in the game."*

During the intro, perform casual **simple cuts, false shuffles, false multiple cuts** — nonchalant, while talking. Place deck face up in front of spectator.

*"From now on I will not touch the cards anymore. Cut anywhere you want and complete the cut to choose where you start dealing from. If you want, you can even cut several times until you're happy. All good? Perfect."*

**Ensure you can see the marks on the back of the top card.**

*"How many players do you want? We can go up to eight players in total."*

As participants join the table, **look at the correct marking zone on the top card's back** to determine the dealer's position. Address the person at that position.

*"I don't want to touch the deck, so please take it and deal the cards yourself, as you would in a normal game. Give their first card to everyone, going around the table, then deal their second card to everyone. You can all look at your cards."*

**Dealer deals regularly.** Guide verbally if needed.

*"Next, you need to 'burn' the top card of the deck, then deal three cards face up; these are called the flop. Good, now 'burn' the next card, then deal the following card face up, this is the turn. Finally, 'burn' the following card then deal the following card face up, this is the river."*

All real Texas Hold'em rules are followed. **Burn top card face down, deal three face up (flop). Burn next card face down, deal one face up (turn). Burn next card face down, deal one face up (river).** Verify with referee at each step. Invite all players to show their cards — the magician has the best hand.

**Clean-up:** Turn three face-down burn cards face up (without changing position), place face down on deck. Gather dealt hands face up, going counterclockwise from dealer. Hold packet face up, **upjog face card, downjog next, alternate through packet. Pull all upjogged cards out, place on others.** Put face down on deck. Deck is back in Mnemonica.

### Key Notes
**Crucial rules:** (1) You are always the last player dealt to. (2) The deck is marked. (3) The dealer position is indicated by the marked deck. No sleight of hand, no memory, no force, no stooge, no hidden accessories. Deck can be examined and the effect can be repeated immediately with a different result.

**Hand strength distribution:** Two pairs (44.0%), Pair (17.3%), Straight (13.2%), Full House (12.1%), Three of a Kind (11.5%), Four of a Kind (1.1%), Flush (0.5%), High Card (0.3%). No straight flush or royal flush possible.

In ~7% of cases, two elements are marked in a zone — you and one other player will have identical best hands. Announce it as a bonus prediction: *"I will receive the best hand, and someone else will have a similar hand."*

In 0.5% of cases (Ace of Hearts with 2 players, or 10 of Diamonds with 3 players), all players receive identical hands — optionally ask spectator to cut again.

To limit marking effort, restrict player choices to 3-5 players (only zones 3, 4, 5 need marking).

Inspired by Ben Joffe, an Australian computer scientist who in 2009 wrote a program to find deck orders where the dealer always wins regardless of cut, for 2-8 players. Each case produced a different deck order, none being Mnemonica. Hedan reversed the question: "In Mnemonica, can we calculate the dealer's position so the last player wins regardless of cut and number of players?" He tested all 364 cases (every cut × every player count) and recorded the dealer position for each, producing the marking table.
