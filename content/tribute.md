Effect: You claim that you are able to shuffle, cut, and deal four aces with one hand while blindfolded in a two-handed poker game. You shuffle, cut, and deal cards into two poker hands. The first three aces are dealt to your opponent's hand, but you fail to deal the fourth ace to them. Instead, you reveal that you kept the last ace for yourself, and it completes a stronger hand: a Royal Flush.

Method summary:

Set-up:
1. Two jokers are removed.
2. At the top of the deck, four Aces, Ten to King of Spades, and one indifferent card are stacked in a specific order (Ace of Spades ends up crimped near the bottom of the stack).
3. Crimp the bottom card of the deck at the inner left corner.
4. Cut the top four cards of the stack to the bottom of the deck, below the crimp, so the Ace of Spades is on top and the Jack of Spades is on the bottom.
5. Give the deck a slight concave bend to make the one-handed shuffle easier.

Method and Presentation:
1. (Patter introduces the performer's magic heroes, Richard Turner and Rene Lavand, and frames the routine as a tribute to them.) Give the deck a two-handed riffle shuffle, retaining the top and bottom stock intact.
2. (Patter says the performer will shuffle and cut to mix the deck before attempting to deal a winning hand.) Give the deck another in-the-hands riffle shuffle, retaining the stack. While squaring the deck, top palm the Ace of Spades in the right hand.
3. (Patter explains the performer will attempt to deal a winning poker hand one-handed and blindfolded.) Immediately after squaring and palming, cut a little more than half the deck to the table and complete the cut with the remainder, keeping the stack somewhere in the top half.
4. Bring both hands to the tabled deck and square it (optional step if flashing is a concern).
5. Pick up the deck with the right hand; as it's handed to the left hand, turn the head slightly and press the palmed card against the eyes with the right hand (this is the "blindfold").
6. Use the left hand to do a one-handed riffle shuffle while retaining the stack (cut below the stack, and make sure the bottom card of the left-hand packet falls above the crimp when weaving).
7. Do a Charlier cut at the crimp card so the stack returns to the top with the crimp at the bottom.
8. (Patter asks the spectator to turn over each dealt card and confirm whether it's an Ace.) Deal the first card to the spectator, then a card to yourself; repeat.
9. (Patter notes a poker hand has 5 cards, so there's still one more chance.) Continue until the spectator's fourth card is a miss.
10. (Patter expresses mock regret that the spectator didn't get the last Ace.) On the spectator's final card (another miss), slowly remove the right hand from the eyes and turn the wrist to reveal the concealed Ace.
11. (Patter reveals the performer needed that Ace to complete their own hand.) Turn the performer's own cards face up one by one to reveal the Royal Flush.

Key Notes:
- After the shuffle in Method step 2, let the thumb extend across the deck and keep the cards slightly unsquared to eliminate suspicious back-and-forth thumb movement while palming.
- During the one-handed blindfolded shuffle/cut, it's possible to secretly glimpse the crimped card out of the corner of the eye if the deck is held close to the body.
- The climax timing is crucial: don't pause too long after revealing the palmed Ace, or the applause will kill momentum for the Royal Flush reveal. Treat it as one extended climax rather than two separate beats, and move briskly from "mistake" to "revelation."
- The stack can alternatively be set from new-deck order via a cull-and-faro sequence (spread, cull five cards to the middle, cull the Ace of Clubs to the top, faro at the middle, cut two cards to the top, crimp the bottom card).
