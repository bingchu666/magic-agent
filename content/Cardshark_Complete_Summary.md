---
title: The Psychotronic Card
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: A card is reversed in the middle of a red-backed deck and set aside untouched. A spectator selects and signs a different card from the same deck. The signed card is placed among four blue-backed Kings and vanishes. When the reversed card is finally checked, it proves to be the signed selection — despite never having been near the Kings.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Two decks: red-backed and blue-backed. From the blue deck, remove the four Kings; arrange back-to-face: Black King, Black King, King of Diamonds, King of Hearts. Case the remainder. Red deck requires no advance prep.

Method and Presentation:
1. Hold four Kings face-down in right-hand Biddle grip, red deck in left dealing grip. Turn right palm up to show Kings (King of Hearts held only by second finger/thumb tips).
2. Turn right palm down; square Kings onto red deck. **Secretly unload the King of Hearts onto the red deck:** press down so the spread bends concavely; the face King springs free. **Square the remaining three Kings;** run left thumbtip along edge. Table three-King packet at inner left corner. Case blue deck.
3. **Spread through red deck faces toward yourself** to find King of Hearts. Table it face down; **insert face down into middle of the face-up deck.** Ribbon-spread face up.
4. Respread to re-show reversed card. *(Remember, this card was reversed before the trick even began.)* Close spread; **obtain a left fourth-finger break under the card below the reversed card. Double undercut** to bring reversed card second from back. **Obtain break above bottom two cards** (King of Hearts + face-up card beneath) by buckling with left forefinger.
5. Take deck in right-hand Biddle grip (thumb takes break). **Swing cut ~⅓ into left hand; peel cards one by one** from face of right packet onto left, asking spectator to call "stop" at any spot card. *(Say "stop" when you see a spot card you like. The only reason for using a spot card is so your signature will show up better.)*
6. Spectator signs the face of the card on the left-hand packet. (Spot card makes the vanish from four picture cards more visual.)
7. **Execute the James/Ellis loading move:** Flip signed card face down with right-hand packet. Place right fingertips on back; **push forward until right thumb hits inner end of lower packet** (card outjogged ~half length). Left hand turns palm down to display selection; simultaneously **left fourth finger takes over the break — two cards stolen onto the face.** (Left hand swings away; right stays stationary to hide stolen cards.) Point to signature with right forefinger. **Left forefinger squares outjogged card** as left hand turns palm up (retention-of-vision illusion). Signed card now reversed in deck; tabled card is King of Hearts.
8. **Thumb off the face-down card** next to King packet. Drop right-hand cards onto left. Place red deck face down at outer right corner.
9. Pick up blue King packet; turn face up. **Elmsley count** to show four Kings. Turn face down.
10. **Obtain break above bottom King via pinky pull-down (or Jennings' heel-down); insert red card into this break.**
11. **False count to show five cards:** Peel top card into dealing grip; peel next while **stealing first back to bottom. Outjog the red-backed card.** Spectators see: blue, blue, red, blue, blue.
12. Square outjog card. *(I will now cause the red-backed selection to vanish.)* Magical gesture. **Elmsley count** to show four blue-backed cards. Pause; turn face up; spread — only four Kings remain. Toss each King onto table, rubbing to emphasize singleness.
13. Gather Kings (King of Hearts second from face). Casually **perform Ed Marlo's Olram subtlety:** thumb off face King into right hand at inner right corner; turn both hands palm down to flash backs, then palm up; toss right card to table, thumb next face card onto it; repeat for remaining two, sliding last two under tabled Kings. Keep Kings at lower left corner.
14. *(At the very beginning I reversed one card and never touched it again.)* Flip red deck face up; **table-spread across outer end.** Pull reversed card injogged. **Perform a domino turnover (Daryl)** — deck flips face down, injogged card turns face up: the signed selection.

Key Notes:
1. **No-Contact Condition:** The mystery card is reversed at outset and never touched until climax. Kings at inner left, red deck at outer end — no possible transfer. Emphasize this separation.
2. **James/Ellis Loading Move:** Original text provides detailed, illustrated description. Critical: left hand swings away while right stays stationary; squaring timed with hand-turning produces retention-of-vision. Independently invented by Wesley James (1973) and Tom Ellis.
3. **Showmanship & Pacing:** Sell the vanish with strong gesture and pause. Final revelation follows briskly — treat vanish + revelation as one extended climax. Avoid overplaying magician-in-trouble.
4. **Clean-Up (Bernard Bilis):** After climax, right hand holds red deck in Biddle grip; left picks up face-up Kings, squares, **pull-down to obtain break above red-backed King of Diamonds.** Take packet in right-hand Biddle grip; pick up cased blue deck. Slide red deck inward — King packet squares onto deck — **release card under break.** No heat; trick is over.

Credits: Based on Jim Swain's "Technicolor Mystery" (Don't Blink), itself from Larry Jennings' "The Mystery Card" and Brother Hamman's "The Signed Card." Additional concepts: Elmsley, Marlo, Goldstein. James/Ellis loading move: Wesley James / Tom Ellis (independent). Clean-up: Bernard Bilis. Domino turnover: Daryl. Olram subtlety: Ed Marlo.

title: Against All Odds
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: A deck in new-deck order is displayed. Turned face down, a spectator freely selects a card. After several square cuts, she inserts her card back around the middle. When spread face up, the card is exactly back in its correct sequential position.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Deck in new-deck order: suits segregated, numbers in sequence (A-K or K-A). No gimmicks. Ideal when opening a new deck.

Method and Presentation:
1. Ribbon-spread face up to show order. Gather; turn face down.
2. **Square cut.** *(This cut changes every card's position but preserves the sequence.)*
3. Spectator gives a **square cut.** *(Now no one can know any card's location.)*
4. Spread between hands; spectator touches any card. **Outjog it and break the spread — it becomes the bottom card of the right-hand portion.** Place face down on table. Square by **placing right-hand cards under left-hand cards** (secretly cuts deck at removal point).
5. **Crimp inner left corner of the bottom card** (facing you). Table deck; **square cut, positioning crimp near middle.**
6. Spectator gives a **square cut.** If she misses crimp, **give one more cut to bring crimp back to bottom.**
7. Hand selection to spectator. **Take deck in right hand by inner end.** She inserts card around middle, outjogged half length.
8. **Execute Bill Simon's "Business Card Prophesy" move:** *(Let us see what card you selected.)* Spread cards; **break directly above outjogged card. Right hand turns palm down; grips outjogged card by outer end, clipping with thumb against deck. Turn palm up; place right-hand cards under left-hand cards.** Card now appears face up, outjogged. In reality, you've cut around the outjogged card. Combined with secret cut (Step 4) and crimp restored to bottom (Step 6), selection is back in original sequence position.
9. **Square up, cleanly pushing outjogged card flush.** *(You could have selected any card and inserted it anywhere — yet you put it back exactly where it belongs.)* (Revisionist history: earlier said "around the middle," now "anywhere in the deck.")
10. Turn deck face up, end over end; **ribbon-spread from right to left** (indices right-side up; end-over-end hides crimp). Card is back in position. **Remove and reinsert face up in same spot** for visual reinforcement.

Key Notes:
1. **"Middle" Instruction:** Inserting around middle is essential — if near top/bottom, after Bill Simon move the card shifts to opposite end.
2. **Bill Simon's Move:** Original provides thorough description. Creates illusion of merely turning a card face up while actually cutting the deck. Smooth, unhurried execution is key.
3. **Why Cuts Matter:** Make coincidence seem far more impossible; prevent discrepancy if spectator selects near the top.

Credits: Plot: Paul Curry's "New View" (Phoenix #120). Method: Darwin Ortiz (free selection + full-view insertion). "Business Card Prophesy" move: Bill Simon.

title: Signature Effect
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Phase 1 — A spectator selects a card and signs the back (identity unknown to all). The card is lost. She then freely names any card from a face-up spread — it proves to be the signed card. Phase 2 — She covers the signed card with her hand, then freely selects another card (shown face and back, no signature). The signature jumps from the card under her hand to the new selection.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Permanent marking pen. Red-backed deck recommended. No gimmicks. Can be performed walk-around with no table.

Method and Presentation:
*Phase One:*
1. Hand pen to spectator. Spread cards face down; spectator touches any card. **Outjog, square, remove; place face down on top.** No one sees face.
2. Spectator signs top card in large letters. (Keep deck in hand.)
3. **False shuffle to retain signed card second from top:** Overhand — pull small group, run one, injog next, finish. Second shuffle — thumb break under injog, shuffle to break, throw rest on top.
4. Turn deck face up. Overhand shuffle: milk off bottom card, throw last ~6 cards on face while **catching left fourth-finger break** under them. Signed card directly above break.
5. *(You selected a card unknown to everyone. Now choose another — this time from a face-up deck.)* Spread until two cards remain above break. **Block push-off these two.** Hold double with right fingers; reposition left thumb on face; press lower card upward with left fingertips to ride it right until contacting right palm. **Slide right-hand spread rightward until hidden card clicks free.** Signed card rides unseen beneath face-up spread.
6. Spectator touches any face-up card. **Allow signed card to enter spread directly under the designated card; close spread** (subtle upward lift of right-hand portion — no visible motion).
7. Turn deck face down. **Spread to find signature.** Break above it; square left-hand cards; **take fourth-finger break under card below signed one** (two cards above break). Place right-hand cards under left from front.
8. Spectator names her card. **Double turnover** — signed card matches her selection.
9. **Kurtz pivot double display (Ortiz modification):** Grip double at outer right corner; slide forward, press left. Left second finger engages inner right corner; right moves right — double pivots face down. Left forefinger pushes corner — pivots face up. Slide forefinger diagonally — pivots face down. Place on top, **catch fourth-finger break.** (Casually displays both sides; gives audience time to absorb.)

*Phase Two:*
10. *(Hold out your hand.)* **Vernon two-card push-off. Execute L.J. move:** Both hands turn palm down and separate — right hand takes under card clipped face up; left draws upper card square. Place face-up card on table before spectator. Left forefinger points to her hand, then tabled card — she covers it. (Pointing justifies palm-down left hand, concealing signed card on top.)
11. *(The truth is stranger: whatever card you select, your signature jumps to that card.)* **Pinky count break under four cards. Double undercut these four from face to back.** Turn deck face down. **Pinky count break under top five.**
12. Spread deck. Push over three singles; **block push-off remaining two; steal signed card under spread** (as in Step 5). Continue spreading. Spectator touches any card with free hand.
13. **Execute MC spread double lift (Mike Close):** Take touched card **under right-hand spread, outjogged half length.** Signed card (riding beneath) now under outjogged selection. **Place right-hand cards on left — upper half outjogged.** Right hand over deck; right fingers slide back to square upper half — outjogged selection slides back, but signed card stays (held by left thumb/forefinger). Right hand grips outjogged card at outer left corner; left fingers slide forward to butt hidden card against right fingers. **Pull double out, face up onto top; catch break.** Announce "selected card" (forced signed card, signature hidden).
14. **Drag double lift (Ken Krenzel):** Grip double at inner right corner; slide thumbtip along right side, letting inner left corner pivot against left thumb base. Turn right palm up to revolve double face down. Point toward spectator's hand. *(The signature is trapped under your hand...)* Flip double face up onto deck; catch break. (Pointing gives audience clear look at unsigned back.)
15. *(You could have selected any card.)* **Execute K.M. move: Vernon two-card push-off** of face-up double. Grip at right edge. **Slide double right while turning left hand palm down.** When left edge meets right edge of deck, turn left fully palm down. **Pull face card right; left fingertips retain back card on top.** Use single card to scrape deck edge as indicatory gesture. Set deck aside.
16. Hold card face up between second finger and thumb. Rub against back of spectator's hand (covering first selection). Build suspense. Snap card face down — signature revealed. Spectator checks under her hand — signature gone. Give signed card as souvenir.

Key Notes:
1. **Under-Spread Placement:** Central to both phases. No shuffling/cutting after selection — what makes first climax play so strongly. Master to invisibility.
2. **MC Spread Double Lift:** Mike Close's creation — simultaneously forces, conceals signature, and sets up double lift. Original devotes several pages. Critical: signed card stays put (held by left fingers) when upper half squares.
3. **Two-Phase Structure:** Kurtz pivot display gives buffer between phases. L.J. move reinforces illusion that signature really is on tabled card.
4. **Walk-Around Adaptation:** No table needed — spectator holds card on palm, covers with other hand. Face-down selection made by fingertip.
5. **Signature Size:** Insist on large letters; if too small, ask them to circle it.

Credits: Starting point: Gary Kurtz's "Psychological Fusion" (Continuations). MC spread double lift: Mike Close (Workers 2). L.J. move: Larry Jennings (modifying Bruce Elliott). K.M. move: Ortiz/Jennings/Krenzel lineage. Drag double lift: Ken Krenzel.

title: Four To One
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: The Ace of Spades is placed on the table; four Kings are placed under a spectator's hand. They change places — the Kings become the Ace (under the hand), and the Ace becomes four Kings (in full view).

Method summary:

Set-up:
Upjog four Kings and Ace of Spades. Stack from top: King of Spades, Ace of Spades, Red King, King of Clubs, Red King.

Method and Presentation:
1. **Double turnover** to show Ace of Spades. Turn double down; deal top card to table as "the ace."
2. Count top four cards into right hand (one under the other), last card onto other three. **Elmsley count while holding the deck** (left forefinger slides deck back; left thumb/first two fingers free for count). Four Kings show; Ace at back.
3. Take four cards face down in Biddle grip. **Obtain break under top card of deck; transfer break to forefinger at outer right corner.** Place four-card packet on deck, outjogged half length, sidejogged right. Rub tabled card against table.
4. **Count packet again:** Take top card, place on bottom ("one"). Take second; turn hand palm down to flash face; **secretly insert into forefinger break** ("two"). Repeat with third and fourth. (Howard Lyons/Larry Jennings technique.) One Ace now on top, three Kings under it, one King on table as "the ace."
5. Grip packet at outer right corner; shift to align sides (still outjogged). **Grip only top card** (cover front edge with fingers to hide lack of thickness). Move forward; left forefinger pushes three cards flush with deck. Place single card before spectator — she covers "four Kings" (actually just the Ace).
6. Overhand shuffle to lose top card; injog/shuffle-off to bring three Kings to top. **Obtain break under three Kings.** Rub tabled card and spectator's hand. **Top palm the three Kings (Erdnase top palm).**
7. Reach to tabled card; hand flat, **palmed Kings square on top. Spread fingers and pat — cards spread inward** (Bernard Bilis). Appears as if four cards were always there. Snap face up → four Kings. Spectator checks under hand → Ace of Spades.

Key Notes:
1. **Passing One as Four:** Tabled card exposed only a fraction of a second — have spectator's hand poised above table beforehand.
2. **Deck Retention:** Never put deck down — it plays no role in effect but is central to method. (Elmsley count with deck in hand: Larry Jennings' idea.)

Credits: Based on Ortiz's "Slick Aces" (Darwin Ortiz at the Card Table), based on Tom Mullica's trick. Bilis spread: Bernard Bilis.

title: The Unholy Three
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Three cards selected from a red deck and signed. Red deck placed under spectator's hand. Performer picks up blue deck and cuts to three matching cards — which then prove to be the actual signed, red-backed selections.

Method summary:

Set-up:
One blue-backed card secretly on bottom of red deck. Blue deck shuffled by spectator, placed to left. Marking pens ready.

Method and Presentation:
1. Spread red deck (concealing blue card). Three spectators each select and sign a card.
2. **Thumb count bottom five; take fourth-finger break. Running cut** to table until break; sidejog last five. Square, taking break under sidejogged packet (blue card 5th from top).
3. **Hofzinser-type spread control:** Push over three singles; **block push-off two cards above break. Draw top of double back; push bottom of double right until it butts against right palm** (aligns blue card with top). Draw spread right until blue card clicks free. Blue card rides under spread.
4. Break spread ~¼ down. First selection replaced on left-hand cards; push over; right-hand spread on top. **Left fingers straighten to push selection flush against right palm.** Draw right until card clicks free. Two cards now ride under spread.
5. Repeat at ~½ and ~¾ for second and third selections. Three selections on bottom; blue card directly above.
6. **Thumb count bottom four; take break. Angle these four cards** (held by index corners between right fourth fingertip and thumbtip).
7. **Vernon transfer:** Left hand grips red deck at left side (covers angled cards). Both hands move toward spectator. Red deck passes over blue deck; **right hand picks up blue deck in Biddle grip — angled cards coalesce on top.** Left hand places red deck before spectator; she covers it.
8. Jog shuffle preserving top four. **Out-faro shuffle** (only top 4 need perfect interlace). Three red-backed selections at 3rd, 5th, 7th from top of blue deck.
9. Three tabled slip cuts; each followed by **double turnover** revealing matching card. After each: necktie second deal / **L.J. move** to table face up without flashing red backs. After third: **K.M. move** as gesturing. Ribbon-spread blue deck.
10. *(Without ever touching the red deck, to find the actual signed cards...)* Turn three selections face down — red backs and signatures.

Key Notes:
Blue-backed cover card prevents any flash of red during faro and handling. Vernon transfer must be smooth and unhesitating.

Credits: Based on Peter Kane's "Divination, Discovery, Departure." Structurally influenced by Rene Lavand's "Perhaps, Some Day."

title: Beyond Sleight of Hand
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Spectator selects a card, buries it herself, and shuffles. Performer buries two red Jacks face up together. While pack rests on table, selected card materializes between the Jacks.

Method summary:

Set-up:
None. Remove two red Jacks; drop face up on table.

Method and Presentation:
1. Spectator shuffles deck. Spread; spectator touches any card.
2. **Jennings' open control:** Break spread above touched card; draw upper half inward. Take selected card under spread, outjogged half length. Raise hands; tilt faces toward audience. Once left-hand top out of view, **place selected card on top of left-hand cards; place card above it square.** Loosen grip — selected card slides down. When jogged ~1 inch, **left forefinger pulls it square** as hand tilts back (retention-of-vision illusion). Thumb top card (apparent selection) face down onto table.
3. Place right-hand cards under left. **Obtain break under top card.** Scoop two Jacks face up onto deck. **Pick up all cards above break in Biddle grip. Peel face Jack onto deck, sidejogged right; take under double, sidejogged left. Square and flex** (convex bend prevents edge flare). Drop Jacks back on table.
4. Cut off ~half deck; **sandwich tabled "selected card" between halves, outjogged.** Spectator squares it flush into tabled deck, then shuffles.
5. Pick up Jacks. Pull lowermost left half width. Spectator tables shuffled deck. *(Bury the Jacks at whatever point you determine.)* She cuts ~half. Pick up lower half under Jacks; square Jacks on top; **take break under double (uppermost Jack + selection).** Drop other half on top; retain break.
6. **Ribbonspread hideout (Charles Nyquist):** Slide cards below break left ~¼ inch (step hidden by left thumb). Square halves but **press left fingertips against bottom card of top half so it sidejogs** (~1 inch). Ribbon-spread left to right — face-up Jacks show together; selected card hidden under spread.
7. Square deck slowly, fairly. Magical gesture. Ribbon-spread again — one card materialized between Jacks. Spectator names card; turn sandwiched card over.

Key Notes:
Test ribbonspread hideout on unfamiliar surfaces first. Presentation sells that everything is spectator-controlled.

Credits: Ribbonspread hideout: Charles Nyquist (Hugard's Magic Monthly, 1948). Sandwiching technique: Juan Tamariz.

title: Blockbuster
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Spectator freely selects a card, buried and lost. Performer asks its color, then causes every card of that color to turn face up — with one exception: the spectator's selection remains face down.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Deck separated by color. 26th card (bottom of upper color) corner-crimped at inner left.

Method and Presentation:
1. Ribbon-spread; spectator draws any card. Note whether from above or below crimped card. Gather deck; **lift at crimp with right thumb; left fourth finger takes break.**
2. If selection from above crimp: **cut about half above break to table; cut remaining above break; drop rest stepped left ~½ inch. Square, taking break at step.**
3. *(Emphasize spectator's freedom.)* **Half-pass all cards below break** (turn toward left; use table edge for cover — everyone watches selection being shown).
4. Turn back. **Double undercut top card to bottom** (avoid flashing face-up cards).
5. **Biased pressure fan (Ed Marlo + Ortiz):** Bevel cards strongly; fan only top half. Backs show ~¾ of fan. Insert selection into center of fan (goes into face-down half). Square.
6. Table for riffle shuffle. Cut just above center (2-3 face-down cards on top of lower face-up half). **Table faro** (needn't be perfect; distributes face-up cards among face-down).
7. *(Would it be a good trick if I could turn your card face up? Instead, something far more difficult.)* Ask color. Magical gesture. Ribbon-spread — every card of that color face up. *(Every single one — with one exception.)* Spectator names card. **Domino turnover** reveals selection face down among face-up opposite-color cards.

Key Notes:
Biased pressure fan creates major time displacement — dirty work before fan, fan convincingly shows nothing changed. Straighten deck afterwards by upjogging face-up cards and stripping out.

Credits: Ortiz considers this a new plot in card magic. The "trapped" element — each reversed card sandwiched between face-down cards — limits maximum to 25 reversals.

title: Nulda's Revenge
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Two initialed cards: one placed in pocket, one on table. Instantly, the pocketed card proves to be the one just seen on the table; the tabled card proves to be the one placed in the pocket.

Method summary:

Set-up:
None. Remove Ace and Jack of same suit. Ace on face, Jack behind it. Deck discarded after setup.

Method and Presentation:
1. Spectator initials Ace (held on deck), then Jack (taken in right hand). **Obtain break under two face cards. Block push-off — take both cards above break as one** (hidden card under Ace). Square against deck face. Set deck aside.
2. Take three cards face down. **Double turnover** to show Jack. Biddle grip; turn palm up/down to show back-to-back. **Turn double face down.** Take top card (apparently Jack); **slip into right front pants pocket** (push deep).
3. Holding double in left hand. Take from above in right. **Erdnase palm change (Ortiz/Adrian Plate):** Turn right palm up — show Ace; pull double back so inner edge presses against thumb. Turn palm down; left hand reaches for double. Left fingertips contact face at inner left corner; continue reaching until upper card ~half into left thumb crotch. **Lower card pivots into right palm** (right fourth fingertip as pivot). Upper card (Jack) clipped between left thumb and forefinger. Drop on table as "the Ace."
4. Right hand reaches into pocket. **Norman Houghton's pocket load:** only first finger and thumb enter; fabric goes between first and second fingers. Rotate hand inward — heel of hand pushes palmed Ace into pocket. Pinch at corner; pull out. Turn face out → Ace. Turn over tabled card → Jack.

Key Notes:
Initialing eliminates duplicate-card suspicions. Produce pocket card instantly — no dramatic pause; double-take reaction is stronger. 100% angle-proof. Ideal for walk-around.

Credits: Erdnase palm change: actually invented by Adrian Plate (Roterberg's New Era Card Tricks). Two-card transposition: S.W. Erdnase. Pocket load: R. Norman Houghton (Ibidem, 1957).

title: New Hitchcock Aces
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Aces placed in T-formation; three cards dealt on each. First ace travels to leader, then the second. Spectator places hand on third packet. Leader packet turned over — four Kings. Four Aces found under spectator's hand.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Remove four Aces; secretly cull four Kings (9th–12th from top). Tabled Aces in order (L→R): Club, Diamond, Heart, Spade (Spade = leader at D).

Method and Presentation:
1. **Obtain break under top two. Vernon's laydown sequence:** Scoop Aces (Club face, Heart back) onto deck. Lift cards above break in Biddle grip. Peel Club face down; peel Diamond face down — deposit right-hand cards. Take Spade as "leader." Use Spade to flip Heart face down.
2. **Pinky count break under top four.** Take top card (Heart); turn palm up/down. **Take three cards above break as one under Heart, stepped left; take next under, stepped left.** Turn palm up — three Aces show (two indifferent cards hidden behind Diamond). Place on deck; clip lower "two" with left thumb; take Heart to A, then next to B, next to C. Spade face up at D.
3. **Pinky count break under top five. In-the-hands slip cut** (top card to 5th). Ace of Clubs on top; Ace of Diamonds 5th; Kings 9th–12th.
4. Turn Spade face down. **Double turnover.** Deal top on Spade at D. Deal next three on C. Peek at C's bottom; miscall as Ace of Clubs. Magical gesture. Pick up C; **glide out second card from face.** Left turns three face up; right turns glide card face up; drop on three. Turn over D — Club + Spade.
5. Turn black Aces face down. **Double turnover.** Deal top on leader. Deal next three on B. Down-and-under deal at B; last card dramatically turned over. Turn over D — Club + Diamond + Spade.
6. **Pinky count break under top three (Kings).** Gather A and B; place aside. **Versa switch (Ortiz/Carney):** Third finger into break; fourth finger presses separated cards down. Scoop up Aces at D; **slide three broken-off Kings under Aces** as scooping. Square aces+Kings against table.
7. Place ace packet face up on deck with **"in-transit action" (Ascanio):** use packet to flip Heart face up, then place on deck — action is motivated. **Block push-off of cards above break; flip entire block face down.**
8. Thumb over top three; drop at D (actually Kings). Deal top three on Heart at A. Pick up A; turn over to show Heart at face. Spectator covers A. **Double turnover;** deal top on D (4th King). Drop aside-cards on deck.
9. Magical gesture. Turn leader (D) → four Kings. Spectator lifts hand → four Aces.

Key Notes:
The in-transit action motivates placing aces on deck. Pre-climax period generates both humor and suspense.

Credits: "O. Henry Aces" plot: Roger Smith (Four-Ace Switcheroo, 1971). Original version based on Wesley James' "L.S.D. Aces." Plot based on Vernon's "Slow Motion Four-Aces."

title: Kartenkunste
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Spectator selects a card; placed under her hand. Using four Queens, performer identifies the suit: one Queen turns face down matching the suit. Rubbed again, that Queen transforms into the selected card. The Queen is found under spectator's hand.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Note top card's suit (ensure spot card). Remove four Queens; arrange face-to-back: Queen matching noted suit, other Queen of same color, two black Queens.

Method and Presentation:
1. Hold Queens face down in Biddle grip. Spread left; turn palm up to show faces. Turn palm down; **unload bottom Queen onto deck** (concave bend — Queen springs free). Table remaining three Queens.
2. Overhand shuffle: undercut half; injog top card (unloaded Queen); shuffle off. **Take break under injog.**

1. *(Something truly impossible — with tangible proof.)* **Lorayne force (Ortiz variation):** Break above bottom card. Right hand grips front end from below; left pulls bottom card inward to clear grip. Right fans deck clockwise — bottom card disappears under left end of fan.
2. First spectator touches any card in fan. Outjog; remove all cards below it; place on table. Square left-hand cards with selected card still outjogged. Take in Biddle grip — **fingertips square card as face comes into view** (visual retention illusion — outjogged card seems to be the face card). Turn packet face up; spectator signs face card.
3. **Double turnover** of two face cards. Deal face-down card to table (hit double lift action; left wrist turn hides packet face). Revolve left hand to face-down dealing position; reassemble deck.
4. **Repeat fan force** for second spectator (forces other side of double-facer). Spectator signs. You have forced both sides of one double-facer on two people; each signed one side.
5. Take double-facer off face in Biddle grip. Left turns palm down; drops cards onto tabled packet; picks up deck. Pick up tabled card under right-hand card, sidejogged left half width. Check "name" of first selection (name other side). **Square two cards against left thumb base; secretly unload face-down card** (concave bend — springs free). Left holding only double-facer.
6. Run left thumb/fingertip along sides (as if squaring two cards). Tilt: show second spectator's signed face; tilt other way: show first spectator's signed face (left hand obscures single edge).
7. *(Fuse two cards into one.)* Place on left palm; squeeze between palms. Drop to table. Pick up by corner; shake — it's one card. Stunned reaction. Hand to each spectator to examine. Tear in half (each side bears half signature, both sides have faces); give halves as souvenirs.

Key Notes:
Creates an "impossible object." Double-faced card shown openly as a created object — audience never suspects it's a magician's tool. For couples, give whole card to woman. The Lorayne force lets spectator see and touch the card's back during selection — crucial convincer.

Credits: Inspired by Gary Kurtz's "Psychological Fusion." First to fuse two cards into a double-facer: independently by Ortiz, Bernard Bilis (different handling), and Doc Eason/Christopher Carter ("Anniversary Waltz"). First card fusion ever: Richard Kaufman (double-backer, 1981). Fusion plot: Wesley James ("Forgery," 1970s).

tags: [cards]

---
Effect: Using only one hand, the performer shuffles, cuts, and deals a game of poker — giving himself a Royal Flush.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Royal Flush (Ten through Ace, same suit) on top in order. Concave bend in deck. Down-crimp inner left corner of bottom card after jog shuffles.

Method and Presentation:
1. Jog shuffles retaining stack. *(My friends won't play cards with me... "only if you had one hand tied behind your back.")* **Down-crimp inner left corner of bottom card** (buckle with left forefinger; right thumb engages buckled card; slide left and crimp).

3. **One-handed riffle shuffle (faro):** Split deck on forefinger; upper half rides over forefinger nail; adjust with thumb/fingers; bevel so only bottom few cards touch; press to interlace corners. Must be a perfect faro for the crimp + 5 cards below. **Charlier cut** at crimp (crimp to bottom). Royal Flush now 2nd, 4th, 6th, 8th, 10th from top.

Extend forefinger over outer end; pull down; release — card springs face up, pivoting around front end. Tilt; release to table. Royal Flush dealt to yourself.

Key Notes:
Corner crimp must be subtle — too strong causes weave discrepancy. Concave bend is essential for one-handed faro. Routine takes ~90 seconds. Can be performed standing with limited table space. Perfect showcase for any one-handed flourishes.

Credits: One-handed riffle shuffle: Howard de Courcy (Hugard's Magic Monthly, 1945). One-handed cut: Neal Elias. First effect to exploit perfect-faro feature of one-handed shuffle: Darwin Ortiz.

props: [cards]

source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Performer shuffles four face-up Aces into face-down deck. Shows Aces distributed throughout, none near top or bottom. Deals four poker hands — Aces dealt to himself from the center. Spectators see face-up Aces materializing out of the face-down deck.

Method summary:

Set-up:
None. Remove four Aces; remember which is fourth from face. Drop face up on deck.

Method and Presentation:
1. *(Demonstrating the center deal — the most legendary technique in card cheating.)* Riffle shuffle: shuffle two cards on top of Aces. **Slip triple cut** (as in "The Sting"). Aces now 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th from top (one face-down card above).
2. **Two table in-faro shuffles** (or out-faro + adjustment via double undercut). Aces now 5th, 9th, 13th, 17th from top.
3. **Pinky count break under 5th card.** *(Notice no aces near the top.)* Thumb over three singles; **two-card push-off of double above break.** Continue two more singles. Display seven face-down cards — no aces visible. Square.
4. Right hand takes deck in Biddle grip; tilt up. Left peels cards off face. *(No aces near the bottom either.)*
5. **Cut to lowermost ace (17th from top):** estimate cut; push over couple cards if needed. Slide three more from upper half; press spread to table; thumb 4-5 below ace. *(Notice no other aces near it — and that's buried deep.)*
6. Square. Deal four-handed poker. **Each time you reach your hand, deal a second** (strike or push-off). Face-up Ace materializes at fingertips each time. Only four rounds needed. Ribbon-spread deck to display.

Key Notes:
The spread showing the ace isolated deep in the deck (despite another ace just 4 cards above) sells the center-deal premise. The combination of (a) convincing evidence aces are buried, (b) aces from different depths, and (c) visual of face-up aces emerging makes this the strongest center-deal simulation.

Credits: Based on Milt Kort's "This is Centers (?)" lecture notes. Ortiz added handling subtleties and presentational elements. Earlier version in Darwin's Deceits (1979).

title: Darwin's Poker Deal

props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: Story of spotting a cheat using daubed cards. Four Aces removed from red deck; spectator signs backs. Red-backed Aces inserted into blue deck. Shuffle and deal: performer receives signed red-backed Aces; opponent gets a straight flush. Climax: performer's hand transforms to a Royal Flush — the signed red-backed cards are now Royal Flush cards.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Blue deck: three Aces (Diamonds, Clubs, Hearts) on top, ten indifferent cards, then straight flush (10-6 of Hearts), rest. Ten of Spades 11th from bottom. Place in case. Red deck: cull King, Queen, Jack of Spades to top during previous effect.

Method and Presentation:
1. **Braue reversal (Lorayne variation):** Break under top three; right cuts half (thumb takes break); left levers bottom face up; left takes cards below break, flips face up. Push out back card of face-up cards; right inserts cards between left's cards and this pushed-out card. Square; turn deck face down. Order: face-down indifferent card, face-up Jack, Queen, King of Spades, rest face down.
2. Place red deck aside face up. Remove blue deck; ribbon-spread face down. Gather; set aside. Pick up red deck; spread; drop Aces on table (don't expose reversed cards). Place red deck face down in dealing grip.
3. **Stripper switch (Ortiz handling):** **Pinky count break under top four.** Pick up Aces face up in Biddle grip (Spade on bottom). Place on deck: packet butts left thumb out of way; thumbtip pulls top card slightly left (exposing outer right corner of Jack). Right fourth finger grips all cards above break; **slide combined packet right half width.** Right second finger slides across outer edge (pulls cover card square). Square packet; spread Aces right; Spade stays square with three cards beneath. Flip entire block face down (right thumb extends along edge hiding thickness). Spread top four; place on table as "the Aces."
4. Spectator signs/marks each "Ace" on back (initials, name, last name, doodle). Subtlety: pick up last card (real Ace of Spades), squint at doodle — flashes face to audience.
5. **Pinky count break under top three** of red deck. Place "Aces" on top. **Zarrow addition:** spread top four; block push-off all above break (7 cards); flip onto deck; catch break; repeat to turn face down. No break this time.
6. Take four red cards; turn face up; grip outer end. Flip deck face up; riffle to Ten of Spades; thumb count one more; insert packet behind Ten of Spades. Turn deck face down.
7. **Double undercut bottom to top (Vernon method). Two perfect out-faro shuffles. Ose's false cut.** Deal four-handed draw poker.
8. Red-backed cards fall to your hand. Each time: **pinky count; double turnover** to show Ace; turn down; deal. On fourth (real Ace of Spades), single turnover. Last card (Ten of Spades) dealt without showing.
9. Scoop your cards; spread face down (first climax: dealt yourself the signed Aces). Turn over opponent's hand — straight flush in Hearts (second climax). Gather first/third hands; bury. Reverse-count your cards; slowly deal face up — Royal Flush (third climax: signed cards transformed).

Key Notes:
Triple climax: dealt Aces → opponent's straight flush → Royal Flush transformation. Faro shuffles handle a 56-card deck — split by feel. Prevent anyone from checking discarded hands (contain blue-backed Aces). The red/blue contrast makes the effect visually undeniable.

Credits: Starting point: Ron Ferris's "The Green Gambler" (Animations, Apparitions, and Aces). Also influenced by Jim Swain's "The Big Stack" (Don't Blink). Zarrow addition: Herb Zarrow.

Secretly get four Aces to top of deck. $50 or $100 bill in easily reachable right-hand pocket.

4. *(You've made all choices. One last choice: exchange hands if you wish.)* Before answer: reach into pocket with right hand; take out cash; drop on table. *(I'm so confident I'll wager this.)* (Note: cash in right-reachable pocket so you don't have to put deck down.)

7. *(Do you want to draw any cards?)* Spectator draws. You stand pat. Spectator shows hand. Turn your hand over; deal one at a time — four Aces. Express surprise; pocket cash.

Key Notes:
Strongest misdirection of any switch — everyone stares at spectator's hand. The option to exchange hands eliminates "he forced the cards" suspicion. The bet raises dramatic stakes. Never agree to deuces wild (increases straight flush chances).

---
title: The Sting
difficulty: advanced
props: [cards]
tags: [cards]
source: "Cardshark, Darwin Ortiz"
---
Effect: From a spectator-shuffled deck: performer deals himself four Aces, then escalates to dealing four Queens to one player, four Kings to another, and four Aces to himself — a triple pat-hand demonstration.

Method summary:

Set-up:
Spectator shuffles at outset. No advance arrangement.

Method and Presentation:
1. Spectator shuffles. Openly remove four Aces; drop in front. Hand deck to another spectator to shuffle again (double-shuffle reinforces memory).
2. Drop Aces on top. Riffle shuffle one card on top. **Slip triple cut** (right forefinger drags top card; right moves forward/right with bottom ⅔; left retains top ⅓ minus top card; cut sequence). Aces back on top, appearing buried.
3. Spectator names number of players. **Riffle stack** Aces for that number (or use Bottom Runup System). Deal face up — Aces to performer.
4. Gather all except Aces. Ensure top card not a King/Queen (peek bottom; shuffle to top). Drop Aces on top; **double undercut top five to bottom.** Riffle shuffle preserving bottom stock. Indifferent card on face; four Aces behind it.
5. *(Working with a partner... stacking lesser hands...)* Turn deck face up; spread; remove four Queens (toss to table). Meanwhile: **hide Aces under face card** (push off 5-card block at start); **underspread cull four Kings** to top.
6. Overhand shuffle: undercut ~half; **slip off face card** (Aces now on face of right half); shuffle onto top; run last ~6 singly. Aces on top; Kings in middle.
7. **In-the-hands faro:** split so Kings are on top of lower half (thumb up inner end to spot last King). In-faro (top down). Kings and Aces alternated on top, King topmost.
8. Drop Queens on top. **Up-the-ladder cut variation:** Right draws top half from under Queens; Queens fall flush on lower half. Right drops cards, sidejogged; pulls out bottom of sidejogged block (¼ deck) to top flush; pulls remaining sidejogged block — **left thumb catches break between lower half and ¼ deck above.** Right slaps cards; cuts all above break right for shuffle.
9. Queens on top of left half; alternated Kings/Aces on top of right half. **Riffle shuffle:** hold back 8+ on right; hold 4 on left; precise drop sequence to achieve the final stack.
10. **Table faro** (or near-faro with adjustment). Deck stacked: Queens to 2nd player, Kings to 4th, Aces to 6th (six-handed game).
11. Deal six hands. *(As promised, I gave the Queens to another player.)* Deal 2nd hand face up — four Queens. *(My partner is over here with four Kings.)* Turn 4th hand — four Kings. Pause. *(Of course, I'm double-crossing my partner with four Aces.)* Turn 6th hand — four Aces.

Key Notes:
Triple climax timing: audience barely absorbs each before next hits. Patter gives logical reason for each pat hand. Audience remembers "picked up shuffled deck, dealt three pat hands" — the pesky details drop from memory. The "sting" concept: audience experiences being conned twice.

Credits: Based on "Black's Stack" (The Vernon Chronicles Vol. 3). Ortiz goal: impromptu handling from shuffled deck + dramatically compelling revelation. Slip triple cut: Darwin Ortiz.
